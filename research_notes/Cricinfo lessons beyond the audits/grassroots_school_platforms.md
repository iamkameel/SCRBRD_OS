# Grassroots, school and junior cricket platforms: coverage, children's data and business models (state as of Oct 2026)

> **Method and confidence.** Researched 29 Oct 2026. This machine's network policy blocked direct fetches of `support.playhq.com`, `play-cricket.ecb.co.uk` and `cricclubs.com` (DNS refused). Everything below comes from search-engine results and their extracts of primary pages (help centres, policies, federation PDFs), plus third-party coverage. Treat the platform-setting details as **medium confidence**: they are quoted from primary help pages, but I could not open those pages in full. No real child's name or photograph is recorded here. Where a search result named a minor, I left the name out on purpose. The earlier audits (`audit/ESPNCRICINFO_*_2026-10-23.md`) already cover Cricinfo's professional match pages, the player profile as a "development passport" (§4.7) and the hierarchy from school to delivery (§4.8). This note doesn't repeat them.

## 1. Cricinfo's lower tiers: youth, schools and SA schools cricket

### Takeaway
Cricinfo covers youth cricket only at the elite, representative end: the ICC U19 World Cups, U19 internationals and named representative sides such as "South African Schools". On those pages it treats teenage players like professionals, with full names, profile pages, feature pieces and birth dates. It doesn't cover school, club-junior or age-group cricket below that level. In South Africa that tier runs on CricClubs (CSA Schools) and SuperSport Schools.

### Cited Findings
- Cricinfo runs a full series hub for each ICC Under-19 World Cup, including 2017/18, 2023/24 and the 2025/26 warm-ups, with fixtures, live scores and points tables. The hubs look like the senior ones. — [Cricinfo U19 WC 2023/24](https://www.espncricinfo.com/series/icc-under-19-world-cup-2023-24-1399722); [U19 WC 2017/18](https://www.espncricinfo.com/series/icc-u-19-wc-2017-18-1116872); [U19 WC 2026 warm-ups](https://www.espncricinfo.com/series/icc-men-s-under-19-world-cup-warm-up-matches-2025-26-1517955)
- Cricinfo publishes features and profiles of players who are minors. One feature calls its subject "the 17-year-old legspinner". It also hosts individual player pages for U19 squad members, which Wikipedia cites as its source. — [Cricinfo features index](https://www.espncricinfo.com/cricket-features?object=7); [Wikipedia, U19 player article citing Cricinfo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharujan_Shanmuganathan)
- Archived U19 World Cup squad lists on Wikipedia, sourced to Cricinfo, give players' birth dates and ages. — [2008 U19 WC squads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Under-19_Cricket_World_Cup_squads); [2016 U19 WC squads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Under-19_Cricket_World_Cup_squads)
- Cricinfo has a team entity called "South African Schools" (team id 3321), with a schedule, fixtures and results page. This is the representative SA Schools XI, not school-v-school cricket. — [Cricinfo: South African Schools team](https://www.cricinfo.com/team/south-african-schools-3321/match-schedule-fixtures-and-results)
- The Khaya Majola Week itself, CSA's U19 schools week, is scored on **CricClubs under a "CSA Schools" club**. The 2023 event ran 17 teams in 4 groups, with ball-by-ball links. — [CricClubs: CSA Schools, Khaya Majola Boys U-19 Week results](https://cricclubs.com/CSASchools/listMatches.do?teamId=189&league=13&clubId=29335)
- Format and 2026 edition: CSA describes 5 × 50-over games, with teams split into two groups of 8 based on their 2024 placings. Pinnacle Schools reports the 2026 week in Stellenbosch, 16–20 December. — [CSA Youth](https://cricket.co.za/youth/); [Pinnacle Schools, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.pinnacle-schools.com/2026/10/23/tuskers-name-an-experienced-khaya-majola-week-team-all-the-sides/)
- SA school-cricket news and match reports come from SA school-sport outlets such as SuperSport Schools News and SA School Sports, not Cricinfo. — [SuperSport Schools News: CSA cricket weeks](https://superschoolsnews.co.za/tournament-csa-cricket-weeks/); [SA School Sports: Khaya Majola Day 3](https://saschoolsports.co.za/khaya-majola-cricket-week-day-3/)

### Inferences
- Cricinfo applies one presentation model to every player it covers. Once a teenager plays at U19 World Cup or SA Schools XI level, he gets the professional treatment: full name, profile, age and features. Nothing in the results suggests Cricinfo treats minors differently, so it is no model for SCRBRD's child-safe public projection. The norm it represents is journalism about elite representative cricket, which is a different legal basis from a school's own platform.
- In SA the "lower tier" belongs to three players: CSA's own scoring app, CricClubs (CSA Schools) and SuperSport Schools (video). Cricinfo isn't one of them. SCRBRD competes with, or must interoperate with, CricClubs and SuperSport Schools, not Cricinfo.
- SA Schools XI selection comes out of the Khaya Majola Week, and that is where Cricinfo's professional-style exposure of a pupil begins. SCRBRD should expect that a few pilot pupils may later have public Cricinfo pages. That publicity is not SCRBRD's, and SCRBRD should not feed it automatically.

### Gaps
- I found no Cricinfo editorial or privacy policy on publishing minors' birth dates or photos, or on consent. Searches turned up nothing.
- I couldn't confirm whether Cricinfo scorecards the Khaya Majola Week or other CSA youth weeks, beyond the SA Schools XI's own fixtures.
- I couldn't confirm whether the CSA Cricket Scoring App is used at Khaya Majola Week. No source ties the two together.

## 2. The grassroots platforms Cricinfo doesn't cover

### Takeaway
Every federation-backed platform lets club or league volunteers score, and publishes scorecards publicly by default. They differ in the junior-privacy tool. PlayHQ (Australia, NZ, Scotland) has a guardian-set "Private Player" switch that hides the name on every public page. Play-Cricket (ECB) has an admin "display name" field and parent-linked records for under-13s. CricHeroes and CricClubs are open, self-serve platforms whose terms push the problem of minors onto "guardian supervision", and on CricClubs profile content is public. SA schools cricket sits on CricClubs (CSA Schools) and the CSA scoring app, with SuperSport Schools auto-filming matches.

### Cited Findings

**ECB Play-Cricket (England and Wales)**
- Scorecards show only a player's first and last name. Club admins can set a **Display Name** "to differentiate between players of the same name… e.g. parent and child… or for safeguarding purposes". The real name stays visible to club admins and, where the player is registered with a league, to league admins. PCS Pro lets the scorecard name be edited. — [Play-Cricket: Player Names on Play-Cricket](https://play-cricket.ecb.co.uk/hc/en-us/articles/115005866765-Player-Names-On-Play-Cricket); [PCS Pro scorecard name](https://play-cricket.ecb.co.uk/hc/en-us/articles/360001254097-How-do-I-shorten-edit-the-player-name-on-my-scorecard-PCS-Pro)
- Children **under 13 have no login of their own**. Their record hangs off a parent's account, and the parent can edit it. — [Play-Cricket: add a child to a parent's account](https://play-cricket.ecb.co.uk/hc/en-us/articles/360001098418-How-to-add-a-child-to-a-parent-s-account); [Members Administration](https://play-cricket.ecb.co.uk/hc/en-us/articles/115003515085-Members-Administration)
- The privacy statement lists the data held: full name, parent or guardian's name and email where applicable, address, date of birth, contact number, email, gender, batting style, Twitter handle and **photo**. — [Play-Cricket Privacy Statement](https://www.play-cricket.com/privacy)
- I found no documented public/private toggle for junior profiles. Two separate search passes came back empty. — [search result summary, Members Administration](https://play-cricket.ecb.co.uk/hc/en-us/articles/115003515085-Members-Administration)
- ECB publishes a template consent form for juniors playing in open-age cricket (March 2025) and recreational safety regulations (Feb 2025). — [ECB template consent form, juniors in open-age cricket](https://resources.ecb.co.uk/ecb/document/2025/03/19/4e8db050-22a2-41f2-9e39-ab498270ac6f/Template-Consent-Form-Juniors-Playing-in-Open-Age-Cricket.pdf); [ECB Recreational Cricket Safety Regulations, Feb 2025](https://resources.ecb.co.uk/ecb/document/2025/02/14/a7ea3086-d146-406a-ae78-dd6d0e7c5748/Recreational-Cricket-Safety-Regulations-Feb-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The industry norm is public by default.** Scorecards carry real names, and protection is an opt-in, per-person switch (PlayHQ) or an admin workaround (Play-Cricket's display name). SCRBRD's rule of no child's name and no child's photo on a public page goes further than any federation platform found here. That is a selling point to schools and parents, and it needs saying plainly, because volunteers used to Play-Cricket or CricClubs will expect names on the public scorecard.
- **PlayHQ's "Private Player" is the closest precedent**, and a useful pattern: the guardian owns the switch, admins still see the name, and public pages show a placeholder. SCRBRD inverts the default, so every child is private unless the guardian consents. The placeholder idea carries over, for example "Batter 3" or initials with the school's consent.
- **Under-13s without their own login, linked to a parent (Play-Cricket)**, matches SCRBRD's guardian-link model and is a defensible age line to borrow. POPIA itself sets no digital age of consent (see §3).
- **The self-serve platforms (CricHeroes, CricClubs) say minors need guardian supervision and stop there.** In practice anyone can type a child's name into a scorecard, and on CricClubs profile content is public. A South African school pupil at Khaya Majola Week therefore already has public ball-by-ball under his name on CricClubs. SCRBRD must not assume the child's data is unpublished elsewhere, and shouldn't import or mirror CSA Schools/CricClubs data into public views without the same consent gate.
- **Auto-filming by SuperSport Schools** means video of SA pupils is already widely public. A SCRBRD feature that links a moment to a SuperSport Schools clip would join a child's identity to footage. Treat any such link as consent-gated.
- **Breaches in cricket come from third-party satellite systems** (a coaching site, a social network, cloud storage), not the core scorer. SCRBRD's vendor list (Firebase and any OCR provider for the scorebook importer) is where the risk sits.

### Gaps
- PlayHQ's default privacy setting for juniors (Public or Private): the sources conflict, and the page was blocked.
- Play-Cricket: whether any public or private toggle exists for juniors, and whether photos of juniors are blocked or allowed on player pages. Not found, and the pages were blocked.
- CricClubs' and CSA Schools' handling of minors' profiles and photos on CSA Schools pages specifically. The page was blocked.
- Cricket Ireland's platform: no result returned.
- The SuperSport Schools privacy policy's section on children and its consent process: not retrieved.
- I found no reported Information Regulator (SA) action involving a sports platform.

## 3. Child-data norms in sports apps: ECB, Cricket Australia, CSA, the ICO and POPIA

### Takeaway
Every norm found agrees on four points: written parent or guardian consent before a child's image is taken or published, including livestreams; screening of the people who film children; the most protective settings by default (the ICO); and, in South Africa, a ban on processing a child's data unless a s35 ground applies, normally prior consent from a competent person. Cricket Australia and ECB guidance controls **images**. None of the federation sources found says names must be suppressed. That is where SCRBRD goes beyond the federations.

### Cited Findings
- **ECB photo, video and live-streaming guidance:** consent must be sought "from each individual before capturing, streaming, sharing or publishing their image, including posting it on the Cricket Organisation's website, **Play-Cricket** or on social media". For under-18s, "the consent must be sought from the parent / carer". — [ECB Photo, Video and Live Streaming Guidance (Play-Cricket attachment)](https://play-cricket.ecb.co.uk/hc/en-us/article_attachments/20584654975901)
- ECB also publishes "Social Media, Online Communication and Online Safety" guidance (Nov 2023). — [ECB, Nov 2023](https://resources.ecb.co.uk/ecb/document/2024/01/11/cf2dcb7b-8346-4cd1-a589-5d3629fb176f/Social-Media-Online-Communication-and-Online-Safety.pdf)
- **Cricket Australia:** under the *Looking After Our Kids Code of Behaviour*, staff and volunteers may not "take or store photos or footage of Children or Young People except in accordance with this Code". The Code has a dedicated "Photographs and Footage" section. — [CA Code of Behaviour](https://play.cricket.com.au/community/resources/player-safety/child-safety/code-of-behaviour); [Cricket NSW copy, Dec 2024](https://resources.cricket-nsw.pulselive.com/cricket-nsw/document/2024/12/11/de00fc53-ef68-417f-acf5-7bab7c14b957/Australian-Cricket-s-Looking-After-Our-Kids-Code-of-Behaviour.pdf)
- CA: clubs and associations "should obtain informed and **written consent** from the parent or carer" before **livestreaming** matches involving children. Consent may come by digital or paper form, SMS or email. **Photographers, videographers, editors and livestreamers** are roles that need child-safety screening. — [Australian Cricket's Policy for Safeguarding Children and Young People (Nov 2024)](https://resources.playcommunity.pulselive.com/playcommunity/document/2024/11/27/9ddd3384-e1e0-4488-9d3a-dc25eaeefa71/Australian-Cricket-s-Policy-for-Safeguarding-Children-Young-People.pdf); [CA Safeguarding Children Policy page](https://play.cricket.com.au/community/resources/player-safety/child-safety/policy)
- An older (c. 2020) version of CA's policy listed taking photos of children without a parent's written consent, and distributing them, as prohibited conduct. — [CA policy, 2020-era copy](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5973e3fa1b631b9a768a36e8/t/5e267d9263ef4f758b8efaec/1579580835007/Australian+Cricket%27s+Policy+for+Safeguarding+Children+and+Young+People.pdf)
- **CSA Safeguarding Policy (Aug 2025 upload):** it includes annexures for **applications to photograph or interview children** and **parental or guardian consent forms**. The National DSO, or a safeguarding partner the federation appoints, checks clubs' safeguarding compliance every year. — [CSA Safeguarding Policy PDF](https://cricket.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/CSA-Safeguarding-Policy.pdf); [CSA Policies](https://cricket.co.za/policies/)
- **CSA accreditation privacy notice:** CSA treats an identifying photograph and any information about a person under 18 as **special** personal information. It processes minors' data only with parent or guardian consent and in line with its Child Protection Safeguarding Policy. It keeps photos and ID numbers for the season or event plus **24 months**, then deletes them. — [CSA Accreditation Privacy Notice](https://accreditation.cricket.co.za/legal/privacy-notice)
- SA comparator, SA Hockey's safeguarding policy: no photography in changing rooms or toilets, and press or professional photographers must apply and get consent from the **child, the parent or guardian, and the DSO** before taking photos. — [SAHA Safeguarding Policy, Aug 2023](https://sahockey.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/SAHA-Safeguarding-policy-updated-29082023-V1.pdf)
- **POPIA s34–35:** s34 prohibits processing a child's personal information. s35(1) lists the exceptions: (a) prior consent of a **competent person**, normally a parent or guardian; necessity for a right or obligation in law; international public law; historical, statistical or research purposes; and information the child deliberately made public with a competent person's consent. Under s35(2) the Regulator may authorise processing in the public interest with safeguards. A "child" is anyone under 18. — [Acts Online: POPIA s35](https://www.acts.co.za/protection-of-personal-information-/35__general_authorisation_conc); [Michalsons on the Regulator's guidance note](https://www.michalsons.com/blog/guidance-note-on-processing-of-personal-information-of-children/50832)
- The **Information Regulator's Guidance Note on Processing Personal Information of Children (1 June 2021)** spells out the conditions, including that, at a competent person's request, the responsible party must give "a reasonable means for that person to review the processing and **refuse further processing**". — [Guidance Note, lawlibrary.org.za](https://lawlibrary.org.za/akn/za/doc/guidance-note/inforegsa/2021-06-01/guidance-note-on-processing-of-personal-information-of-children/eng@2021-06-01/source); [Acts Online copy](https://www.acts.co.za/popia-gn-children-2021/)
- POPIA sets no fixed digital age of consent. Unlike GDPR's age bands, the line is legal competence. — [Bird & Bird, Children in the Digital World: South Africa](https://childreninthedigitalworld.twobirds.com/home/south-africa)
- **UK ICO Children's Code (Age Appropriate Design Code):** it applies to services "likely to be accessed by children", not only services aimed at them. Settings must start at **high privacy by default**, with **geolocation off by default** and an obvious sign while tracking is active, and **profiling off by default** unless there is a compelling reason in the child's best interests. In **2026** the ICO began a review of children's privacy in mobile gaming, focused on default settings, geolocation and targeted advertising. — [Freeths, 2026](https://www.freeths.co.uk/insights-events/legal-articles/2026/ico-launches-scrutiny-of-children-s-privacy-practices-in-mobile-gaming/); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/uk-ico-launches-review-of-childrens-privacy-in-mobile-gaming); [ICO children's code summary for teachers](https://cy.ico.org.uk/media2/migrated/4019820/5-background-for-teachers-childrens-code-summary.pdf)
- **Defaults on the platforms for under-18s:** Play-Cricket shows the real first and last name on scorecards unless an admin sets a display name. PlayHQ shows the name unless the guardian selects Private, and the default is contested. CricClubs makes profile content public. CricHeroes asks only for guardian supervision. — see the §2 sources: [Play-Cricket player names](https://play-cricket.ecb.co.uk/hc/en-us/articles/115005866765-Player-Names-On-Play-Cricket); [PlayHQ private profiles](https://support.playhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/23971940444572-Private-Profile-settings-for-Participants); [CricClubs privacy](https://cricclubs.com/privacyPolicy.do); [CricHeroes privacy](https://cricheroes.com/privacypolicy)

### Inferences
- In SA, **CSA's own practice already treats any under-18's data and photo as special personal information, held only with guardian consent, for a set retention of season + 24 months**. That fits closely with SCRBRD's stance, and SCRBRD can cite it to schools as the federation's own standard. The 24-month retention is a concrete benchmark for SCRBRD's account-lifecycle and retention design.
- SCRBRD's existing safeguarding design relies on the **CSA Safeguarding Policy's DSO role and its photo-application annexure**. Any SCRBRD photo feature should route through a DSO approval like the one SA Hockey uses (child, guardian and DSO). It shouldn't rest on a guardian toggle alone.
- Read for SCRBRD, POPIA s35 makes **guardian consent (s35(1)(a)) the working basis** for anything beyond the school's own records. The Regulator's "review and refuse further processing" condition maps directly onto revocable consent with a visible audit. The "deliberately made public" ground needs a competent person's consent too, so a child's own public post doesn't let SCRBRD republish it.
- The ICO code isn't SA law, but its defaults (high privacy, geolocation off, profiling off) are the best-articulated benchmark. They bear on SCRBRD's lift clubs (location), the intelligence feed and analyst views (profiling), and the notifications to children.
- The federations focus on **images and streaming**, not names. Name suppression is SCRBRD's own, stricter policy. Expect schools and the CSA Schools/CricClubs ecosystem to challenge it ("names are already public on CricClubs"). The answer: SCRBRD is the school's system, and the school is the responsible party.

### Gaps
- The text of the ECB guidance (date, version, any rule on naming a child next to his photo) and of CA's "Photographs and Footage" section: the pages were blocked or not extracted.
- CSA's rules for photographing schoolchildren at CSA Schools weeks, beyond the annexure titles: not retrieved.
- I found no SA Information Regulator enforcement or guidance on sport specifically.

## 4. Business models: grassroots platforms and Cricinfo

### Takeaway
There are three models. **Federation-funded platforms** (Play-Cricket, PlayHQ) are paid for through registration fees and payment-processing margins. **Freemium consumer apps** (CricHeroes) run on Pro subscriptions plus heavy in-app advertising. **League SaaS** (CricClubs) sells tiered portals plus pay-per-match streaming overlays. In SA school sport, video is free to watch and paid for by the broadcaster (SuperSport Schools, MultiChoice). Cricinfo is an ad-funded media site.

### Cited Findings
- **PlayHQ fees:** a CA support page lists a **1.49% participant service fee**, paid by the player, and a **1.80% merchant fee** from the club's share. PlayHQ's current pricing page shows per-registration rates of **AUD 10 / 8 / 6**, depending on size and features, plus a 1.99% participant fee and a 1.99% merchant fee. The two sources conflict, and the CA page is about 16 months old. — [CA: PlayHQ Fees](https://playcricketsupport.cricket.com.au/hc/en-us/articles/13062834071055-PlayHQ-Fees); [PlayHQ Pricing](https://get.playhq.com/pricing)
- **Australian national registration:** the fee is set nationally by age and locked for three years (2023/24 document). It includes a **AUD 4.80 PlayHQ component**, and CA committed **AUD 35M a year** to grassroots cricket. Cricket NSW cites an AUD 36.50 first-registration fee. — [Queensland Cricket PlayHQ player information 2023/24](https://westsdcc.cricket/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/QC-PlayHQ-Training-Player-Information-Summer-23-24-3.pdf); [CCNSW](https://ccnsw.com/index.php/playhq/)
- **PlayHQ ownership:** sports that invested paid for their equity, and the company doesn't disclose its financials. A reported AUD 90M development spend was called "a fraction of industry rumour". — [CB Insights / press quote](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/assemble-sports)

### Inferences
- In SA a school-cricket product **cannot charge viewers for video**: SuperSport Schools gives it away under a broadcaster's subsidy. SCRBRD's revenue has to come from the school (a per-school or per-team licence, like CricClubs' portal tiers) or from a federation or union contract (like PlayHQ's built-in registration fee), not from parents paying to watch.
- **CricHeroes shows the trap**: a free consumer app reaches tens of millions of users and then depends on programmatic ads. With most users minors, POPIA s34–35 and ICO-style profiling-off defaults make behavioural advertising to child users essentially unavailable. Contextual sponsorship that the school controls is the safe end (a school's kit sponsor on its own pages, nothing targeted).
- A **Pro tier sold to pupils** (CricHeroes-style personal stats and insights) raises consent questions, since a minor would be buying and generating profiling data. Selling it to the guardian or the school is cleaner.
- The **NZ PlayHQ migration** shows that federation contracts win distribution but can alienate clubs, through lost hardware compatibility and missing features at launch. A one-school pilot that may later need a provincial union or CSA Schools to adopt it should plan for importing from and exporting to CricClubs and the CSA app.

### Gaps
- Cricinfo's revenue mix (ads, ESPN+ and subscriptions, video): not researched in this pass. The earlier audits don't cover it either.
- Play-Cricket's funding (it appears to be ECB-funded and free to clubs, but no source was retrieved).
- The CSA Cricket Scoring App's commercial model and the terms of SuperSport Schools' contracts with schools: not found.
- I found no evidence of team-photo or merchandise add-ons on any of these platforms.

## 5. What parents and schools value (reviews and surveys)

### Takeaway
The evidence is thin. What exists points to **reliability and continuity** as the deciders. NZ clubs revolted over a forced platform switch that broke their hardware and dropped features (stats, DLS, tablet scoring). SuperSport Schools' 2.3/5 rating rests on stream lag. I found no survey of parents' privacy preferences for cricket apps.

### Cited Findings
- NZ grassroots players and clubs called PlayHQ's 2022 launch a "shambles". They complained of missing tablet scoring, no DLS, no leaderboards or player stats, and incompatibility with the electronic scoreboards and iPads they had bought for CricHQ. Several associations stayed with or went back to CricHQ. — [Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/nz-news/350499921/cricket-kiwi-grassroots-players-vent-frustrations-as-new-scoring-system-labelled-shambles-as-season-begins); [NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/sport/community-cricket-lashes-out-over-nz-crickets-new-scoring-system/SA23CV75YLUURCZQA2QZECONA4/)
- SuperSport Schools: rated 2.3/5 across more than 250k downloads (third-party aggregator). Google Play reviews complain the stream is "constantly lagging, repeating what has already played". — [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/supersport-schools/1563944026); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.univtec.supersports.app&hl=en_ZA)
- A peer-reviewed study of parents' and players' views of CSA's Hub programme in Western Province touches on safety only in general terms. It says nothing about data or apps. — [PMC, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12200342)

### Inferences
- For SCRBRD's pilot, **offline-safe scoring that never loses a ball, and a live score that doesn't stall**, will matter to parents more than features. That backs up the earlier audits' priority on sync and the Match Centre. The NZ case says migrating from an existing workflow (paper, the CSA app or CricClubs) must not strand the school's existing kit or data. The scorebook importer is part of that answer.
- Parents' privacy preferences are unmeasured in this sector. SCRBRD should collect its own evidence in the pilot, for example consent uptake rates and how many guardians choose to show names, rather than assume.

### Gaps
- I found no parent or school survey on cricket or junior-sport app privacy, and no aggregated app-store review analysis for CricHeroes, CricClubs, Play-Cricket or PlayHQ. A dedicated review-mining pass would be needed, and the review sites may also be blocked here.

## 6. Lessons for SCRBRD (synthesis)

### Takeaway
SCRBRD's private-by-default public projection is stricter than every platform found and is backed by CSA's own treatment of minors' data. That is a defensible selling point. The risks lie at the edges SCRBRD doesn't control: CricClubs/CSA Schools and SuperSport Schools already publish pupils' names and video, and third-party vendors are where cricket breaches have actually happened.

### Cited Findings
- The precedent for guardian-owned name hiding is PlayHQ's "Private Player". — [PlayHQ](https://support.playhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/23971940444572-Private-Profile-settings-for-Participants)
- The precedent for linking under-13s to a parent with no login of their own is Play-Cricket. — [Play-Cricket](https://play-cricket.ecb.co.uk/hc/en-us/articles/360001098418-How-to-add-a-child-to-a-parent-s-account)
- The SA federation's benchmark is CSA's accreditation notice: minors' data and photos are special, processed with guardian consent, and kept for the season + 24 months. — [CSA notice](https://accreditation.cricket.co.za/legal/privacy-notice)
- The statutory basis is POPIA s35(1)(a), and the Regulator's 2021 note requires a means to review and refuse further processing. — [Guidance Note 2021](https://lawlibrary.org.za/akn/za/doc/guidance-note/inforegsa/2021-06-01/guidance-note-on-processing-of-personal-information-of-children/eng@2021-06-01/source)
- Written guardian consent is needed before livestreaming, and people who film children are screened. — [CA Safeguarding Policy 2024](https://resources.playcommunity.pulselive.com/playcommunity/document/2024/11/27/9ddd3384-e1e0-4488-9d3a-dc25eaeefa71/Australian-Cricket-s-Policy-for-Safeguarding-Children-Young-People.pdf)

### Inferences
1. **Keep private-by-default, and put a placeholder on public pages** (PlayHQ's "Private Player" pattern), so a scorecard still reads correctly without names. Make the guardian the owner of any "show name" consent, with the version and time recorded and revocable (POPIA s35 and the Regulator's 2021 note).
2. **Borrow Play-Cricket's under-13 line**: no pupil login under 13, with the record managed through the guardian. Above 13, give the pupil a limited own view, which SCRBRD's existing designs already do.
3. **Adopt CSA's retention benchmark (season + 24 months)** as the default for photos and identifiers in the account-lifecycle design, and cite CSA's own notice to schools.
4. **Route any photo or video feature through the DSO**, following the CSA Safeguarding Policy's photo-application annexure and SA Hockey's three-way consent of child, guardian and DSO, not a parent toggle alone. Treat livestreaming as needing written guardian consent, per CA's policy.
5. **Don't mirror or import CricClubs/CSA Schools or SuperSport Schools content into public views.** Their publicity isn't SCRBRD's consent. Any export to them, for example a fixture to CSA Schools, should go through the same consent check.
6. **Revenue from the school or a federation, not from viewers or ads.** Free broadcaster video (SuperSport Schools) rules out paid viewing. The child-heavy user base rules out behavioural ads. A per-school licence (CricClubs-style tiers) or a union or CSA contract (PlayHQ-style built-in fee) fits. Allow only a sponsor the school controls, shown contextually, with no targeting, tracking or profiling of child users (the ICO's profiling-off default).
7. **Vendor risk is the real breach surface** (ECB's icoachcricket, CricketSocial, BCCI cloud storage). The incidents-and-breaches design should cover Firebase, OCR and any streaming partner.
8. **Win adoption on reliability.** NZ's PlayHQ backlash and SuperSport Schools' ratings show that lost balls, stalls and stranded hardware cost more goodwill than missing features.

### Gaps
- Whether any SA provincial union or CSA Schools would accept a names-suppressed feed for its own records. A question for Kameel or CSA, not answerable from public sources.
