## Who scores: live capture, the people and tools, speed, and checks against official scorers

### Takeaway
Cricinfo publishes almost nothing about its live data operation. No primary source could be found on who enters its ball-by-ball, with what tool, at what latency, or how it is reconciled with the official scorers. The only operational descriptions are anecdotal: a forum post says commentary is written off TV in the Bengaluru office. Other claims sit on spam-grade pages. The governing bodies' official ball-by-ball is collected by **Opta (Stats Perform)** and, in England, **CricViz** analysts. That is the industry's real "official scorer" pipeline, and Cricinfo sits downstream of it or beside it.

### Cited Findings
- **The founding model was volunteers relaying scores.** Cricinfo began in 1993 with Simon King, a British researcher at the University of Minnesota. It started as an IRC bot that shared scores over IRC, Usenet and email lists, then moved to a Gopher server at Minnesota and later to the web ([Wikipedia: Cricinfo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricinfo); [Limited Overs, "Cricinfo's Minnesota roots"](https://limitedovers.com/2013/06/18/cricinfos-minnesota-roots/); [Cricket Stalker, "CricInfo – the early history"](https://cricket-stalker.blogspot.com/2008/06/cricinfo-early-history.html); [81 All Out interview with Simon King, 2022](https://www.81allout.com/2022/11/28/the-first-program-i-ever-wrote-was-cricinfo-simon-king/); [Dave Liverman's History of CricInfo, Medium](https://medium.com/@simon.king0006/dave-livermans-history-of-cricinfo-1a8d300109e8)).
- **Live text commentary is written off TV in the office.** One cricket forum user says "most of their commentary is done by sitting in front of a TV in their office in Bangalore". The same thread suggests commentators may travel to matches that are not broadcast, and that some games may rely on APIs that read the ICC's own live scoring. This is **unsourced hearsay** ([CricketWeb forum](https://www.cricketweb.net/forum/threads/what-is-the-criteria-for-being-a-commentator-on-cricinfos-match-commentary.78024/)). A Quora thread asks the same question ([Quora](https://www.quora.com/How-does-Cricinfo-or-any-other-site-provide-live-commentary-of-any-sport-in-written-work-There-is-a-certain-gap-between-the-actual-events-and-written-update-How-does-that-exactly-work-out)). Its answers were not retrievable.
- **The "150 accredited scorers" claim is unverified.** A claim of "over 150 accredited scorers" using a proprietary "Cricinfo Live Feed" that updates "every few seconds" appears only on a spam-like GitLab-hosted page. **It is not reliable and should not be repeated as fact** ([low-reliability page](https://gitlab.hesconet.com/US/academia/VZlSXRlVONnYHpEaPZlVVRGSCZkWFlTYSxGcWVFbkNVZHR2VWFDb00EbShlVrpFblVkVzlVV5cnYWZFeXtmVXdFRGhFVrh2SNxmWQRlaOd1YUZlVZZlTTFWMKZkYHhXaUJjUydlVkFmUVFzbTxmUPVVMKhUVyQ3bWZFZwEGRKRlVxo1RahFcPJ2RGBlYGRmWjxmW2YFbWNVYxYUWNZlVpJFMaV3VVh2Rh1mRUVVb0NlVXJ1cZVFdPJGbWRjUWpVU)).
- **Each ball carries a short text line and the running score.** Current ball-by-ball pages show each delivery with a short description and the running score, for example Pakistan v Sri Lanka in the October 2026 tri-series ([Cricinfo ball-by-ball page](https://www.cricinfo.com/series/pakistan-odi-tri-series-2026-27-1550706/pakistan-vs-sri-lanka-5th-match-1550717/ball-by-ball-commentary)).
- **Opta collects the boards' official ball-by-ball.** Stats Perform calls itself the world's largest cricket data provider and the official data partner of the ECB, Cricket Australia, **Cricket South Africa**, New Zealand Cricket and the ICC. It says it collects detailed ball-by-ball on over five million deliveries a year across formats. For NZC, its analysts do the official collection and distribute it to the team, media and betting markets ([Intergame](https://www.intergameonline.com/sports-betting/news/optas-hold-on-cricket-data-extended); [Stats Perform cricket](https://www.statsperform.com/team-performance/performance-solutions-for-cricket/)).
- **CricViz collects in the ground.** It says it collects over 1 million balls a year in person, including ball type, shot selection and fielding data. It has been the ECB's official data partner since the 2022 English season, with analysts at more than 500 matches a summer. Its scorecard database goes back to 1877. It was founded by a former Opta analyst and is owned by Ellipse Data ([CricViz data collection](https://cricviz.com/data-collection/); [SportsPro](https://www.sportspro.com/analysis/sponsorship-marketing/ipl-cricviz-ellipse-data-player-analysis-broadcast-sponsorship-fantasy/); [Swarajya interview](https://swarajyamag.com/sports/swarajya-speaks-to-cricviz-the-team-behind-the-data-intelligence-models-for-cricket-world-cup-2019)).
- **Hawk-Eye has fed Cricinfo's live coverage.** An older Cricinfo article says "Hawk-Eye has now been fully integrated into our live match coverage", giving ball speeds and partnership totals ([Cricinfo, "Hawk-Eye at your fingertips"](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/hawk-eye-at-your-fingertips-617079)).

### Inferences
- **Cricinfo's live record is a media product.** It is an editorial transcription of a match, typed by staff watching the broadcast or reading a board's or Opta's feed. It is not the official score. The authority sits with the match's official scorers and, at international level, the board's data partner. SCRBRD's position is the reverse, and stronger. For a school fixture, **the SCRBRD scorer's event log can be the official record** when the school appoints the scorer. The provenance model should say so explicitly: an `official` / `unofficial` / `imported` source on each match, and the appointed scorer's identity on the record.
- **The industry splits roles that SCRBRD can merge, but should label.** Cricinfo and its suppliers separate the person who records the *fact* (the scorer or analyst) from the person who writes the *story* (the commentator). SCRBRD's volunteer does both on one phone. Keeping the event fields, the fact, separate from any free-text note, the story, preserves the split. A text correction then never changes the score, and a score correction always goes through amendments.
- **Opta is Cricket South Africa's official data partner.** That matters if SCRBRD ever feeds or reads CSA pathway data. School data is not covered by those feeds, which is SCRBRD's space.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found for: Cricinfo's scoring software, staff numbers and rotas, publish latency, or any documented reconciliation with official scorers or the match referee's sheet. Fetching Cricinfo's careers pages, or interviews with its editors (Sambit Bal, the stats editors), would be needed, and those hosts were blocked.
- It is not established whether Cricinfo licenses Opta's or the ICC's feed for internationals, or scores independently.

## Corrections and provenance: fixing a ball or scorecard, versioning records, disputes with official figures

### Takeaway
No published Cricinfo correction policy or changelog for scorecards or records was found, and nothing shows that Cricinfo marks a scorecard as amended. Its best-documented "corrections" are archive-level reclassifications: first-class status, and old totals that differ between Wisden, the ACS (the Association of Cricket Statisticians and Historians) and Cricinfo. These show that cricket's records are versioned by *which authority's ruling you follow*, not by an audit log.

### Cited Findings
- **Cricinfo follows the ACS and CricketArchive on Grace, not Wisden.** Cricinfo lists W. G. Grace with 124 first-class centuries, matching CricketArchive. Wisden's long-standing tally was 126, and some matches are counted as first-class by some sources only ([Wikipedia: List of first-class centuries by W. G. Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_first-class_cricket_centuries_by_W._G._Grace)).
- **The same split shows in other players' figures.** A commentator says the ACS and Cricinfo agree with each other but differ from Wisden for Hobbs, Rhodes and Sutcliffe. The root problem is that there is no consensus on which matches were first-class ([CricketWeb, "197 or 199?"](https://www.cricketweb.net/197-or-199/)).
- **Wisden aligned with the ACS in its 2022 edition.** Grace's totals fell from 54,896 runs and 2,876 wickets to 54,211 and 2,809, because the ACS deems ten of his matches not first-class. The lowest first-class team total changed from 12 to 6, after an 1810 match at Lord's ([Wisden, "The great accord"](https://www.wisden.com/wisden-cricketers-almanack/wisden-aligns-with-acs-great-accord-almanack)).
- **Wisden has generally resisted retrospective changes, with one large exception.** It rejected the ICC's 1993 decision to strip first-class status from the South African rebel tours. It did adopt a retrospective change for South African provincial matches from 1971-72, adding 223 matches to the first-class record ([Wisden, "Setting the records straight"](https://www.espncricinfo.com/wisdenalmanack/content/story/251213.html)).
- **The ICC also re-rules status after the fact.** In November 2021 it retrospectively gave women's cricket first-class status ([Wikipedia: First-class cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_cricket)).
- **Ask Steven is a channel for readers' queries, not a correction log.** Steven Lynch's weekly column, Cricinfo's longest-running regular column, answers mostly statistical and trivia questions ([Cricinfo: Ask Steven](https://www.cricinfo.com/genre/ask-steven-6); [The A to Z of Cricinfo](https://www.cricinfo.com/story/the-a-to-z-of-cricinfo-1360915)). No evidence was found that it records amendments.
- **ESPN has a general help page titled "Scoring & Stat Corrections".** Its content could not be retrieved ([ESPN Support](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000099732-Scoring-Stat-Corrections)).

### Inferences
- **The records world has three layers of truth:** the event (what happened), the official score (what the scorers signed), and classification (what the match *counts as* under which authority). Grace's figures moved by tens of wickets without any ball being re-scored. For SCRBRD, this argues for keeping **classification as data apart from the events**: competition, fixture class (competitive, friendly or inter-house) and record eligibility, each versioned with who ruled and when. Re-ruling a fixture's status should then change derived records without touching the event log, and the records page should say "as classified by <authority> on <date>".
- **SCRBRD's audit trail outdoes the industry's best-known site.** No public evidence shows Cricinfo telling readers that a ball or scorecard was changed. SCRBRD's amendments with an audit trail would surpass Cricinfo on trust. It could show "corrected" with the time and reason on a public scorecard, and keep both versions.
- **Disagreement between sources is normal and should be modelled.** When an imported paper scorebook and a live SCRBRD log disagree, the CricketWeb and Wisden pattern suggests recording both and stating which is authoritative, rather than silently overwriting.

### Gaps
- There is no documented case of Cricinfo correcting a live ball or a recent scorecard, and nothing on how it handles a dispute with an official scorer, a match referee or a board. Retrieving one would need Cricinfo pages or news coverage, which were blocked or not found.
- Nothing was found on whether Statsguru or records pages carry a "last updated" or version marker.

## Records and archives: building the history, uncertain data, and coverage labels

### Takeaway
Cricinfo's archive was built **by volunteers typing published scorecards** from print sources such as Wisden. Statsguru grew from that database through volunteer Travis Basevi. The Wisden Almanack archive from 1864 was later folded in after Wisden's ownership. Coverage is uneven. Rich ball attributes (line, length, shot) exist for only a few thousand matches, mostly since about 2014, with large gaps even there. No official Cricinfo coverage label ("ball-by-ball" versus "scorecard only") or rule for unknown values could be confirmed through search.

### Cited Findings
- **Volunteers keyed the archive from print.** Early volunteers typed older scorecards, match reports and the Laws from print references such as Wisden. Simon King organised the teams and designed the database and site structure ([Wikipedia: Cricinfo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricinfo); [81 All Out](https://www.81allout.com/2022/11/28/the-first-program-i-ever-wrote-was-cricinfo-simon-king/)).
- **Statsguru began as volunteer work.** It grew out of the historical scorecard updates and is credited to Travis Basevi, an early volunteer ([Wikipedia: Cricinfo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricinfo); [Wikipedia: Travis Basevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Basevi)).
- **Every Test scorecard was online by 1996.** A 1996 Cricinfo guided tour says the site held scorecards for every Test ever played ([Cricinfo guided tour, archived on imgci](https://i.imgci.com/link_to_database/HELPFILES/WWWHELP/GUIDED_TOUR.html); [Cricinfo archive index](https://i.imgci.com/db/ARCHIVE)). Statsguru today covers matches from the 18th century to the present ([The A to Z of Cricinfo](https://www.cricinfo.com/story/the-a-to-z-of-cricinfo-1360915)).
- **Ownership: Wisden, then ESPN, then JioStar.** The Wisden Group acquired Cricinfo in **2002** according to the Wikipedia snippet (often cited as 2003; **the dates conflict**), and sold it to ESPN in 2007 as the Wisden Group broke up ([Wikipedia: Cricinfo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricinfo)). JioStar has owned it since the November 2024 merger (see Context).
- **The Wisden Almanack is online from 1864.** Cricinfo hosts "Online archive: 1864–2025", with international scorecards and reports; the 2004-onward editions cover each calendar year's internationals ([Cricinfo: Wisden online archive](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/online-archive-1864-2025-292356); [Wisden Almanack hub](https://www.espncricinfo.com/wisdenalmanack/content/story/almanack)).
- **CricketArchive is the comparable archive.** It offers about 900,000 scorecards from 16,000 grounds, was founded in 2003 by Philip Bailey and Peter Griffiths, and has been paid-only since 2017. Records lists typically cite CricketArchive or Wisden as their source ([Wikipedia: CricketArchive](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/CricketArchive); [CricketArchive](https://cricketarchive.co.uk/index.html); [Wikipedia: First-class records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_first-class_cricket_records)).
- **Rich ball attributes are sparse.** A Kaggle dataset built from Cricinfo commentary gives these figures. Only about **4,000 matches** have the detailed line, length, shot and wagon-wheel attributes, and not every match has all four. Line and length exist from about **2014**, and shot names before 2014 are coarser. Overall, line and length are recorded for about **47.5%** of balls and shot for about **74.9%** ([Kaggle: The Ultimate Ball-by-Ball Cricket Dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/ariadaikalam/the-ultimate-ball-by-ball-cricket-dataset)). These are a third party's measurements, not Cricinfo's figures.
- **The Records section is broad.** It covers Tests, ODIs, T20s, first-class, List A, Under-19, women's and "minor cricket" ([Cricinfo: Cricket Statistics](https://www.espncricinfo.com/ci/content/stats/index.html)).

### Inferences
- **Cricinfo's archive is the closest precedent for SCRBRD's photo importer.** Both put people turning paper scorebooks into structured scorecards. Cricinfo's lesson is a weakness: three decades on, sources disagree and there is no visible per-scorecard provenance. SCRBRD's importer can do better from day one. Each imported match should carry its source (photo, OCR, reviewed by whom, when), and each field should carry a confidence of known, inferred or unknown.
- **Unknown must stay distinct from zero.** The Kaggle coverage figures (47.5% and 74.9%) show that even Cricinfo's live data has large holes in its rich fields. SCRBRD should store those fields as nullable with an explicit "not recorded" state. Strike rates and economies should be computed only over balls where the denominator is known, and rate figures should carry "n of N balls recorded". This matches the existing audit's rule against showing "—" as 0.
- **Coverage tiers should be first-class data.** SCRBRD naturally has three tiers: live ball-by-ball from the pad, imported over-by-over from a scorebook, and result or scorecard only. A coverage field on each match should drive which charts and records the match may feed, as in the existing audit's "data coverage" filter chip.

### Gaps
- No snippet confirmed how Cricinfo marks unknown dismissals, missing balls faced or minutes (for example "-" in the B column), partial scorecards, or a coverage label on match pages. These need a direct look at old scorecards, for example 19th-century Tests, which was blocked.
- Nothing was found on the size or staffing of the current records team, or its methodology pages.

## Data model and products: ball fields, Smart Stats, Impact, Forecaster, and licensing

### Takeaway
Cricinfo's ball record holds the outcome and text plus, since about 2014, line, length, shot type and wagon-wheel position, and Hawk-Eye speed where available. Its derived-metrics family, **Superstats** (Smart Stats, Impact, Luck Index and Forecaster), was launched in March 2019 with **IIT Madras and Gyan Data**. It is explained through worked examples, but its weights are not published, which is the main criticism. Cricinfo has **no official public data API**: ESPN closed its public API, and access is through unofficial endpoints and scrapers. Licensed ball-by-ball feeds are sold by Opta and CricViz.

### Cited Findings
- **Fields captured.** Line, length, shot and wagon wheel appear in Cricinfo-derived datasets ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/ariadaikalam/the-ultimate-ball-by-ball-cricket-dataset)). Hawk-Eye speed was integrated into live coverage ([Cricinfo, Hawk-Eye](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/hawk-eye-at-your-fingertips-617079)), and Hawk-Eye tracks line, length, movement and speed ([Cricinfo, "A threat or an advantage?"](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/a-threat-or-an-advantage-250359)). Cricinfo used length data in analysis, for example "What is the best length to bowl in Tests?" ([The Cricket Monthly](https://www.thecricketmonthly.com/story/1154076/what-is-the-best-length-to-bowl-in-tests)). Unofficial API listings expose wagon-wheel, scorecard and live-feed endpoints ([parse.bot listing](https://parse.bot/marketplace/561edfa7-ccf4-4edd-86de-fb726095f6e1/espncricinfo-com-api); [GitHub scraper](https://github.com/ahmedmuqaddas/ESPNCricInfo-BallByBall); [python-espncricinfo](https://pypi.org/project/python-espncricinfo/)).
- **Superstats: launch, builders and parts.** It launched in March 2019 and was built by IIT Madras researchers and the IIT-M-incubated Gyan Data with Cricinfo's cricket experts, derived from Cricinfo's ball-by-ball database. It bundles Smart Stats, Luck Index and Forecaster ([Cricinfo, "Launching Superstats"](https://www.cricinfo.com/story/launching-superstats-the-new-language-for-cricket-analysis-1178276); [InsideSport](https://www.insidesport.in/espncricinfo-launches-new-tool-to-calculate-probabilities-in-cricket/); [IndianWeb2](https://www.indianweb2.com/2019/03/espn-ties-with-iit-madras-for-ai-that.html); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/sport/a-new-era-begins-for-cricket-stats-where-quality-of-opposition-pressure-will-count/210428/); [Adgully](https://archive.adgully.com/espncricinfo-launches-superstats-a-new-metrics-84395.html)).
- **Smart Stats.** It adjusts runs and wickets for pitch conditions, opposition quality and match situation ("pressure"), giving Smart Runs, Smart Strike Rate, Smart Wickets and Smart Economy. Every performance gets an **Impact** value, compared against a replacement-level baseline. Later versions add a Player Quality Index and Pressure Indices. Cricinfo's own FAQ concedes that Smart Stats **does not account for fielding** ([Explainer: how Smart Stats works](https://www.espn.com/espn/print?id=26312449); [Smart Stats: the HD version](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/smart-stats-the-hd-version-of-conventional-cricket-stats-1178323)). It is explained through cases, such as "A tale of two KL Rahul hundreds" and why Sam Curran's Impact beat Moeen Ali's ([Cricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/smart-stats-a-tale-of-two-kl-rahul-hundreds-1312281); [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/cricket/story/_/id/31291228/csk-v-rr-ipl-2021-smart-stats-why-sam-curran-was-impact-was-higher-moeen-alis); [Russell MVP](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/_/id/26632147/smart-stats-russell-mvp-hardik-pushes-pollard-top-batting-spot)).
- **Luck Index.** It identifies each "lucky event", such as a dropped catch, and values it in runs by simulating the opposite outcome ([Cricinfo, "Launching Superstats"](https://www.cricinfo.com/story/launching-superstats-the-new-language-for-cricket-analysis-1178276); [Insights IAS summary](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2019/03/25/superstats-a-new-metrics-to-analyse-the-game-of-cricket/)).
- **Forecaster.** It gives a chasing side's win probability ball by ball and projects first-innings totals. Its inputs are team batting and bowling strength, batter-versus-bowler records, phase strike and economy rates, momentum over the last six balls, and the historical win rate from that position. Cricinfo claimed it picked the winner about **60%** of the time at the start of IPL 2016–18 chases, rising to about **80%** by the 15th over. That is **a self-reported figure with no independent validation** ([InsideSport](https://www.insidesport.in/espncricinfo-launches-new-tool-to-calculate-probabilities-in-cricket/); [Punekar News](https://www.punekarnews.in/espncricinfo-launches-superstats-new-metrics-that-uses-data-science-to-analyze-the-game-of-cricket/)). In one 2018 example the model gave Rajasthan 41% where the historical rate was 60% (same sources). WASP is the earlier comparable model ([Wikipedia: WASP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WASP_(cricket_calculation_tool))).
- **The main criticism is opacity.** A Flashscore analysis says Impact stats "stand behind a rather opaque wall – the guarded algorithms" and have had little uptake in the game's common language ([Flashscore, "Relative runs"](https://www.flashscore.com/news/cricket-the-ashes-relative-runs-a-new-way-of-finding-value-in-cricket-s-batting-statistics/0bjwDREd)).
- **No official API.** ESPN closed its public API and stopped issuing keys ([App Developer Magazine](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/the-world-wide-leader-in-sports-leaves-developers-in-a-world-of-hurt-as-espn-closes-public-api/)). Secondary sources give the closure date as **2014** or **2018**, and **the dates conflict**. Vendors state that Cricinfo publishes no documented developer API, and that unofficial data carries no right to commercial use ([APISports](https://apisports.net/providers/espncricinfo); [PublicAPIs](https://publicapis.io/espn-sports-api)).
- **Licensed feeds come from Opta and CricViz.** Opta sells ball-by-ball to media and betting, and runs models such as Next Ball Predictor and Live Win Probability ([Stats Perform](https://www.statsperform.com/team-performance/performance-solutions-for-cricket/); [Flowics SDAPI connector](https://support.flowics.com/en/articles/8873590-working-with-data-connectors-stats-perform-sdapi-cricket)). CricViz licenses to broadcasters, teams and the IPL ([SportsPro](https://www.sportspro.com/analysis/sponsorship-marketing/ipl-cricviz-ellipse-data-player-analysis-broadcast-sponsorship-fantasy/)). Cricsheet is an independent open source of ball-by-ball data ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/ariadaikalam/the-ultimate-ball-by-ball-cricket-dataset)).

### Inferences
- **Derived metrics are only as trusted as their explanation.** Cricinfo's worked-example articles work. Its unpublished weights draw the main criticism. For SCRBRD, any figure derived from a child's balls, such as a "pressure" rating or a par line, should publish its formula and inputs. It should be recomputable from the event log, and it should go stale and recompute when an amendment lands (the GA-I36 "corrections everywhere" design).
- **Win probability for school cricket would be a poor fit.** Forecaster leans on large historical samples. SCRBRD has small, heterogeneous school samples and minors involved, so a "Forecaster" would be poorly calibrated and emotionally loaded. A transparent par line (DLS-style resources) is the safer analogue, and the ground-display design already has one.
- **SCRBRD's fields should be optional and tiered.** Line, length, shot and wagon wheel are costly to capture, and Cricinfo has them for under half its balls even with paid staff. For volunteers on phones, they should be optional fields with explicit coverage flags. Mandatory data should stay limited to Laws-relevant events.
- **The licensing lesson.** Cricinfo's data is widely scraped and has no licence, which is a cautionary tale. If SCRBRD ever exposes data, it should be through a documented, permissioned API that never exposes minors' identities, rather than leaving scrapers to fill the gap.

### Gaps
- No current (2025–26) Smart Stats or Forecaster methodology update was found. The sources date from the 2019–22 launch period.
- No independent statistical critique of Impact or Forecaster calibration was found. The Flashscore piece is the only critique located.
- Cricinfo's own data supply (in-house versus Opta or ICC feeds) and any current Hawk-Eye contract remain unconfirmed.

## Lower-tier and domestic matches: how coverage differs for associate, domestic and junior games

### Takeaway
Cricinfo lists associate and minor competitions with "Live scorecard" links. Examples are the CWC Challenge League and Canada's NCL GT20. Its Records section includes Under-19 and "minor cricket". No source stated what level of coverage these get, ball-by-ball text or scorecard only, or what Cricinfo does when it holds only a scorecard. A forum claim, unverified, says commentators may travel to unbroadcast matches or read ICC live scoring.

### Cited Findings
- **Associate fixtures get scorecard pages.** The results and live-scores index lists the CWC Challenge League and NCL GT20 Canada with "Live scorecard" links, for example "Toronto Kings 153/6 (20 ov)" ([Cricinfo results](https://www.cricinfo.com/live-cricket-match-results); [Cricinfo weekly results](https://www.cricinfo.com/ci/engine/match/index.html?view=week)).
- **A help-style guide describes domestic T20 pages.** It says domestic T20s show ball-by-ball scorecards that update automatically, with a link to full commentary. This is a general description, not a tier policy ([Drawpie guide, third party](https://drawpie.com/blog/espncricinfo-live-scores-guide/)).
- **The Records section includes Under-19 and "minor cricket"** ([Cricinfo: Cricket Statistics](https://www.espncricinfo.com/ci/content/stats/index.html)).
- **For unbroadcast matches, commentators may travel or rely on ICC live-scoring APIs.** This is an unsourced forum claim ([CricketWeb forum](https://www.cricketweb.net/forum/threads/what-is-the-criteria-for-being-a-commentator-on-cricinfos-match-commentary.78024/)).
- **Most of the history is held as scorecards only.** CricketArchive's roughly 900,000 scorecards show that most of the game's history exists only as scorecards, not ball-by-ball ([CricketArchive](https://cricketarchive.co.uk/index.html)).

### Inferences
- **School cricket is a gap no major site fills.** Cricinfo's depth falls away below associate and domestic T20, and no evidence suggests any junior school coverage. SCRBRD's school-level ball-by-ball is therefore a niche the major sites leave empty. The trust bar should be set by the official scorebook, not by Cricinfo.
- **A scorecard-only match should state its tier.** The coverage-tier field proposed above lets a scorecard-only school match (an imported paper book, or a result phoned in) appear on the same page with an honest label. Ball-level charts and records should be withheld for it.

### Gaps
- No source states Cricinfo's per-tier coverage policy, a "scorecard only" label, or the fallback when only a result or scorecard arrives. Viewing a minor-match page directly would answer this, but the host was blocked.
- Nothing was found on Cricinfo's handling of junior or schools cricket in South Africa, for example Cricket South Africa's U19 Week or Coca-Cola Khaya Majola Week.

## Trust signals shown to readers: sources, "as at" times, provisional results and DLS notes

### Takeaway
No source documented the trust signals Cricinfo shows: source credits, "as at" timestamps, provisional or official result flags, or DLS footnotes on its scorecards. The governing conventions are clear, though. A DLS result must be labelled "(DLS method)". The target can be revised mid-innings. The DLS report must be kept even when the match ends as no result.

### Cited Findings
- **A DLS result must carry its label.** Under the DLS conventions, if a match is decided by DLS, this "must be noted and shown after the result", for example "Team 2 won by 11 runs (DLS Method)" ([Cricket NSW DLS v4.0 handout](https://nswcusa.cricketnsw.com.au/app/uploads/2020/05/DLS-v4.0-2020-Instructional-Handout_202204.pdf); [DLS Application Convention 2022](https://nswcusa.cricketnsw.com.au/app/uploads/2020/05/DLS_Application_Convention_2022.pdf)).
- **The DLS report is kept, and the margin confirmed.** If a revised target has been set, the DLS report must be kept even if the match is later declared "no result". The operator enters the final match information and confirms that the winning margin is correctly recorded ([Cricket Ireland DLS3 protocol, 2018](https://cricketireland.ie/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/DLS3_protocol_September_2018.pdf)).
- **Targets can be revised during a chase.** In one T20I, Bangladesh began the chase before the revised target was official. Play stopped at 0-12 after 1.3 overs, the target was set at 170 from 16 overs, and it was later revised again to 171 ([Wisden](https://wisden.com/cricket-news/t20i-delayed-in-chaotic-circumstances-after-bangladesh-begin-chase-without-dls-target)).
- **Cricinfo publishes DLS explainers.** For example, "Why Kings XI had to chase just 125" ([ESPN/Cricinfo DLS explainer](https://www.espn.com/cricket/story/_/id/23282635)).
- **Smart Stats are explained case by case.** The worked-example articles cited above act as a methodological trust signal for the derived metrics.

### Inferences
- **Targets and results are versioned events.** The Bangladesh case shows a target can change twice in an innings. SCRBRD should treat a target or result as a versioned event (provisional, then revised, then confirmed), not a field that gets overwritten. The scorecard should show the DLS label and keep each revision in the audit. This fits SCRBRD-114, one model of playing conditions read by the pad, the fold, the Laws check and SQL.
- **SCRBRD can show the trust signals Cricinfo leaves undocumented.** Candidates: "Scored live by <role> · last ball <time>", "Imported from scorebook photo · checked by <role>", "Result confirmed by <coach or teacher in charge>", and "Corrected <time>: <reason>". These cost little and follow from SCRBRD's event log and amendments.
- **Public pages should name roles, not children or volunteers.** Under `docs/policy/PUBLIC_DATA.md` and the minors-data designs, a public trust signal should name a role, such as "school scorer", rather than a pupil scorer's name.

### Gaps
- Cricinfo's actual scorecard footnotes were not confirmed: DLS notes, "match referee" and "TV umpire" listings, "result confirmed" or provisional states, "as at" times, and "scorecard courtesy of" source credits. A direct look at a current scorecard is needed, and the host was blocked.
- No evidence was found of Cricinfo flagging a scorecard as provisional pending the official scorers, or as amended after the match.
