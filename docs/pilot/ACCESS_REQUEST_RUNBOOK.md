# When a family asks for everything the school holds about their child

**For the school's information officer and the office.** Paper steps for the pilot, before the app has a request button (slice 0 of `docs/design/ACCESS_REQUESTS.md`; Kameel answered the design's 15 questions "as recommended" on 30 Oct 2026). Section numbers of POPIA and PAIA below are the design's reading of them: **to confirm with counsel**.

The school answers, not SCRBRD. The school holds the child's record; SCRBRD runs the app for it. Write no child's name in the request entry, in a file name or in a message. Use the entry's short code.

---

## 1. A request comes in

Write the date of receipt on the request. The clock starts when the request is complete: you know who is asking, which child, and have seen proof of standing (step 2).

- Anything that reaches SCRBRD instead of the school (support, an email to Kameel) comes to you within **5 working days**, and the asker is told: "Your request is about records held by the school. We have passed it to the school's information officer on [date]; the school will answer you."
- A request about the **sign-in account** (the name and email the person signs in with, and sign-in times) is SCRBRD's, not the school's. Pass it to Kameel; he answers it himself.

## 2. Who may ask, and how you check

Check standing and identity **before anyone opens the child's record**.

| the asker | what you do |
|---|---|
| a parent or guardian whose link in the app is live and verified | ask for photo ID; confirm the link in the app (**Family**) |
| a parent whose link is pending, refused, ended or removed | standing by documents: ID, and the child's birth certificate or a court order. You decide. Do not say why a link is not live |
| the pupil himself, **eighteen or over** | photo ID; he may have his whole pack, including who has read his record |
| the pupil **under eighteen** | he does not ask alone. A guardian asks. What the app shows him in **Me** is his already, and the office may print it for him on request |
| a former pupil | photo ID; the office reads his record as the school still holds it |
| a lawyer, grandparent, new partner or anyone acting "for the family" | written authority signed by a person in the rows above, and that person's ID. The pack goes to the person who signed, not the agent, unless the authority says otherwise |
| another parent asking about someone else's son, another school, a union, a league | this is not an access request. Pass a worry about a child to the Designated Safeguarding Officer; say nothing else about whether the child exists |

Two parents who disagree each ask separately and each gets her own pack. Neither can block the other. Do not choose between them. If a court has said something, record what it said.

## 3. The DSO checks every request

Every request, without exception, goes to the school's Designated Safeguarding Officer (DSO) before release. He reads the lines that will go in the pack (messages, notes, availability comments) and does one of two things:

- writes "nothing to add"; or
- crosses out a line, which you then replace with `[a line withheld · PAIA s63]`.

He writes no reason on the request. He cannot refuse a request or hold it past the due date; his check has **3 working days**. If he thinks the asker should not have the record at all, that is a matter for the Safeguarding Policy (the office ends the link with a recorded reason), not for this runbook. He initials the entry before release.

**The pack must look the same for every child.** A section the school does not disclose is a fixed sentence, word for word, in every pack. Never write "none", a count or a date for a part you hold back. Never leave a section out. A gap that appears only for some children shows a concern exists.

## 4. Who decides

The principal or the office (the information officer decides; the office does the work). **Not the asker.** Not anyone who is, or ever was, a guardian of the child. If the office clerk is the boy's mother, the principal decides. The decision is one of: released, released in part, refused, or passed on (to SCRBRD for account matters; to another school for a boy's new school's records). Where a part is held back, say which ground (§6).

## 5. How fast

| | |
|---|---|
| **The deadline** | 30 calendar days after the request is complete (PAIA s56(1); to confirm with counsel) |
| **The school's aim** | 10 working days. This is the school's promise to families, not the law's |
| **An extension** | once, up to 30 more days (PAIA s57; to confirm with counsel). Only for a very large record or a needed consultation. In writing to the asker **before** the first due date, with the new date. Never silently |
| **What the family is told** | three things only: "Received [date]; the school will answer by [date]", "Ready", and if extended, "The school needs until [new date]". Never that the DSO is checking, or any step |

Confirming that the school holds a record about the child is immediate. It is the same list for every child: roster details, match figures, availability, injury record, consents, notices and messages, public-page status.

## 6. What goes in the pack, and what is held back

Assemble by hand from what the app already shows the family: **Family**, **Me**, **Settings → Who Read What**, and the consents screen. Use the same sections, in the same order, every time.

**In** (the child's own rows, in plain words):

- name, team, role, date of birth, contact details, height and weight; the ID number as "ends [last three digits]; the office will confirm the full number in person" (never the whole number);
- his line in each match (his batting, bowling and fielding, how he was out, the result) and a link to the public scorecard; his season and career figures. Not the full scorecard;
- availability: every answer, who gave it ("you", "the pupil", "the office");
- his own row on the team sheet;
- honours and milestones, with "withdrawn · [date]" where withdrawn (no reason);
- the injury record, in full;
- workload: match overs and breach outcomes; the nets figures only where a share was made to the parent;
- consents: each with its version, date, given or refused, and by whom ("you", "the other guardian", "the pupil"); the fact and date of any never-public mark (not the reason);
- his links: state and dates (never the reason a link ended);
- emergency contacts, his transport and lift seats, notices sent to the parent about him, his child message thread (sender by role), what the public pages show about him today;
- who has read his record, by role, school and kind of record (the names stay with the office; give one in person from **Who Read What** if asked);
- whether he was read as an opposition player, and by which school;
- whether his account is active or disabled, and the date; every earlier request about him, its date and outcome; the date of any paper scorebook import.

**Held back, by these sentences exactly** (the design's wording, or Kameel's if he changes it):

| section | the sentence |
|---|---|
| **Safeguarding** (every pack) | "Records kept by the school's Designated Safeguarding Officer, if any exist, are not part of this response. They are held under the school's Safeguarding Policy and Cricket South Africa's, and a request about them is made to the Designated Safeguarding Officer, whose card is in the app." |
| **Coaching notes** (every pack) | "Coaches may write notes for themselves and the school's coaching staff about a player's development. The school does not disclose these notes through a request for access; they are the coaches' working opinions and are kept for two years after a pupil leaves. If you believe a note is wrong, you may ask the information officer to review it." |
| **Conduct** (a parent's pack) | "Records of conduct, where any exist, are communicated to a pupil's family by the school in its own way and are not part of this response." |
| **Wellness check-ins** (a parent's pack) | "Daily check-ins a pupil records about how his body feels are shown to him in the app. The school shares them with a parent by a recorded act for a stated period; the shares made to you are listed below. Nothing else of them is in this response." |
| **Other children** | "Other pupils appear in this response as the school's public pages would show them. Their records are their own families' to ask for." |
| **Other adults** | "Staff and other adults appear by their role. Their contact details and the school's records about them are not part of this response." |
| **SCRBRD's own records** | "SCRBRD, which runs this app for the school, holds the account you sign in with: your name, email address, sign-in method and sign-in times. A request about those is made to SCRBRD's information officer at [the address on the printed card], and is answered within the same time." |
| **A part refused** | "A part of the record is withheld under section [63] of the Promotion of Access to Information Act. You may ask the Information Regulator to review this decision; its details are at the end of this response." |

A pupil's own pack (eighteen or over) also has his skill scores, his conduct record and the school's log of who read his record. It still holds back coaching notes and safeguarding.

**Other children, by public label only.** Show another boy as the public page would: initial and surname if his family has agreed to that, otherwise his position ("the opening bowler"). Even if the parent saw his full name on the screen: a paper in a bag is not the app. "c [name]" in a how-out becomes "caught". The other ten on a team sheet, other children's messages, lift passengers and side threads stay out.

**Other adults, by role.** "The U14A coach", "the physio", "the other guardian". A name only where the family was shown it in the app at the time (a message in the child's thread, a driver they accepted). Never an adult's phone number, email, address, account state, or any reason recorded about them.

**Coaching notes are not disclosed.** That is the school's stated position (Kameel, 30 Oct). If the family asks the information officer to review one note, the information officer reads that note and decides whether it is wrong.

**The ground for withholding.** Every pack carries at least one use of the PAIA s63 line (a third party's privacy), so its presence says nothing. Do not use any other ground yourself. If you think one is needed, stop and ask Kameel; counsel has not yet said how it may be worded.

## 7. Fee

None. The request, the confirmation and the pack are free for a family in the pilot. Do not invite one.

## 8. Handing the pack over

- **In person, with ID, against the request's short code.** The best way. Print it, hand it over, write "collected" and the date on the entry.
- **If they cannot come:** a PDF protected with a password; send the file by email with nothing about the child in the subject or the message; give the password by phone.
- **Never** attach an unprotected pack to an email. **Never** put a child's name in a subject line, file name, message or text. Name the file `SCRBRD-record-[short code]`.
- A parent with no account (a former pupil, a parent whose link ended) gets paper or the password PDF.
- Say plainly where a correction can be asked for. If the family says something in the pack is wrong, record it as a separate request (the school corrects a record with evidence; a wrong ball goes to the scorer's amendment route; if the school does not agree, a note that a correction was asked for and not made is attached to the record). Do not change the pack itself.

## 9. What is kept, and for how long

One file per request, on the encrypted disk where the data-event entries live (`docs/pilot/BACKUP_RESTORE.md` §3), passphrase in the password manager. **Never in this repository, never in a chat.** In the entry write the short code, the dates, the kind of asker and the outcome. No child's name, no value from the record.

| what | kept |
|---|---|
| the request entry and decision (who asked in role terms, dates, outcome, the DSO's initials, the information officer's signature) | **7 years** after closing, so the Information Regulator can be answered years later |
| the manifest (which sections, which fixed sentences, how many rows in each, which lines carried the s63 line). Values are not in it | with the entry, 7 years |
| the pack itself, paper and file | **90 days** after release. Then shred the paper and delete the file and every copy. A second copy of a child's record is a second thing to lose |
| the DSO's marked-up lines | with the pack: 90 days |

Entry headings, in the shape of `docs/policy/DATA_EVENT_TEMPLATE.md`: short code; received on; complete on; asker's standing (and what proof was seen); channel; DSO checked on and initials; decided by, on, and outcome; extension (date of notice, new due date); released on; collected on; pack deleted on.

## 10. If a request arrives before the entry template is signed

Answer it anyway. The screens and logs exist. Assemble the pack by hand as above, and type the entry in afterwards, dated when it really happened.

## 11. Checks before release

- [ ] the asker's standing and ID were checked before the record was opened
- [ ] the DSO initialled the entry
- [ ] the decider is not the asker and was never a guardian of the child
- [ ] every section is present, with its fixed sentence where held back
- [ ] no other child by name; no adult's number, email or reason
- [ ] ID number shows only its last three digits
- [ ] no child's name in the file name, subject line or message
- [ ] the due date was kept, or the extension was in writing before it
- [ ] the information officer signed
