## 0 · In one page

A family already reads most of what the school holds about their child: Family and Me show the file, the figures, the injury record, the consents, and (designed) who read him. ACCOUNT_LIFECYCLE §6.4 says so and adds nothing new in code. What is missing is the **formal answer**: one record that a request was made, one pack assembled by a rule rather than by whoever happened to be in the office, one decision by a named person who was not the asker, one timeline the family can see, and one way to say "no" to a part of it that reads the same whatever the reason.

The whole design is seven sentences:

1. **The school answers, SCRBRD does not.** The school is the responsible party for the child's record; SCRBRD is its operator. A request that reaches SCRBRD about a school's child is handed to that school's information officer within five working days and the asker is told. SCRBRD answers only for what SCRBRD holds as responsible party: the account and its sign-ins (D1).
2. **Who may ask is who may read.** A live, verified, consented guardian link, or the pupil himself at eighteen: the app's button is theirs. Everyone else (a parent with no live link, a former pupil without an account, a lawyer, a relative) asks the office on paper, and the office verifies standing by documents before it reads anything. The app's answer to a person with no live link is one fixed sentence, whatever the reason the link is not live (D2).
3. **The pack has the same shape for every child.** Every section appears in every pack; a class the school does not disclose is a fixed sentence that appears in every pack, never a sentence that appears only when there is something to hide. That one rule is what keeps a safeguarding concern from showing through: the pack of a boy with an open concern and the pack of a boy without one are the same shape, line for line, and a test says so (D3).
4. **Other people stay out.** Other children appear as the public page would show them (initial and surname with consent, else the position), never as a person. Adults appear by role and, where the family already saw it on screen, the name as shown; never a number, an email or a reason recorded about them (D4).
5. **Two people release, neither is the asker.** The information officer decides; the DSO marks every request "checked" before release, and his only outputs are "nothing to add" or a line redacted with the same marker every pack uses for third parties. The family sees one status until the day it is ready; nothing in the timing says which step was pending (D5, D6).
6. **Thirty days, aim for ten.** Received, due date, ready: three states the family sees. One extension of up to thirty days, in writing, for volume or consultation only, never silently (D7). Confirmation that a record exists is immediate and free; the pack is free for a family in the pilot (D8).
7. **A correction is an act on the record, never an edit of the pack.** Contacts the family fixes itself; the record the office fixes with evidence; a wrong ball is an amendment (GA-I36); a coach's score is withdrawn or re-rated, never overwritten (AH-D5). Where the school does not agree, a marker is attached to the record saying a correction was asked for and not made, read with the record by everyone who reads the record (D9).

What exists already does most of the work: `selfaccess` and `guardian` bundles, `my_child_access_log()` (INCIDENTS D20, designed), `scoring_amendment`, `skill_withdraw()`, the parent's own edits of contacts, the retention schedule (D22). What this adds is one table for the request, one for the correction, one SECURITY DEFINER that assembles the pack by the table in §4 and logs the assembly as a read, two small screens, a register, and a runbook for the pilot that does all of it by hand from the screens that exist.

## 1 · The decisions

Each is a recommendation until Kameel says. "As recommended" is a valid answer to every question in §7.

**D1 · Responsible party and operator.** The school is the responsible party for everything in classes A–V about a pupil (READERS §1). SCRBRD is the operator. A family's request about their child is the school's to answer; a request that arrives at SCRBRD (support, an email to Kameel) is transferred to the school's information officer within five working days with a note to the asker: "Your request is about records held by {school}. We have passed it to the school's information officer on {date}; the school will answer you." SCRBRD is responsible party only for the **account**: `app_user` (name, email, active), `auth_identity` (the Google sign-in's account number and verified email, per `SIGNIN_PRIVACY_NOTICE.md`), `auth_session`, and support-session records about that account. Those Kameel answers himself, from Me and the owner's reads, logged.

**D2 · Standing.** The table in §2. The rule: the app's self-serve request belongs to the people whose assignment already reaches the child (a live verified consented `guardian` link; `selfaccess` from eighteen). Every other asker goes through the office's paper route, where the information officer verifies standing before anyone reads a row. The app's refusal to a signed-in person with no live link is **one fixed sentence**: "You do not currently have a link to a pupil at this school that lets you request his record through the app. You can ask the school office in writing; the office will tell you what it needs." It is the same sentence for a link that was never made, was refused, has ended, or was revoked, so a revocation the office made for a reason the DSO gave is told to nobody by it (ACCOUNT_LIFECYCLE D13; GA-I20 §4.3).

**D3 · The same shape.** The pack is a fixed list of sections (§4), in a fixed order, for every child at every school on the platform. A section the school does not disclose is present as a **fixed sentence**, identical in every pack. No count, no "none", no date is written for a withheld class. The fixed sentences are in `packages/policy/src/access_pack.mjs` beside the section list, and a test assembles the pack for two seeded boys, one with an open concern naming him and an adult in his household, one with none, and asserts the two manifests are identical in sections, sentences and markers (§6, slice 2's proof). This is the tipping-off control, and it is the only one that works: a redaction that appears only when there is something to redact is itself the disclosure.

**D4 · Other people.** Anything that leaves the platform follows the public rule for other children (PUBLIC_DATA: initial and surname with a live consent, else the position; a never-public boy is the position) **even where the signed-in parent saw full names on the screen** (STEP4 Q4 is about the screen, inside the platform and inside the log; a file on a phone is neither). Adults: by role ("the U14A coach", "the physio", "the school office", "a coach at the other school"), and the name only where the family was shown it on the platform at the time (a message in the child's thread, the driver of a lift she accepted). Never an adult's phone number, email, address, account state or any reason recorded about them (a revocation reason, a disable reason, a moderation reason, a withdrawn honour's reason). The other guardian is "the other guardian", as the consents screen already says (STEP4 §4).

**D5 · Who decides.** A new capability `access.request.decide`, held by `principal` (POPIA's default information officer is the head of the body) and `schooladmin` (the office, as the usual deputy), at school scope. Separation of duties, refused by the function: the decider is not the requester; the decider does not hold a guardian link, live or ended, to the child; the decider is not the subject of any correction request in the same bundle. An office clerk who is also the opener's mother cannot release her own son's pack; the principal does. The decision is one of `released`, `released_in_part`, `refused`, `redirected` (to SCRBRD, for account matters; to another school, for a departed boy's new school's records), each with the PAIA ground where a part is withheld, from a fixed list (§3.3).

**D6 · The DSO's mark.** Every request, without exception, carries a `dso_checked_at` set by a `dso` at the school before release is possible. The DSO's screen lists open requests with the child's name (he reads children's names by standing) and the free-text lines the pack will carry (the availability notes, the child-thread messages, the notices' bodies, the trip rows). His only acts: **"Nothing to add"**, or **redact a line**, which replaces it in the pack with the same marker D4 uses for every third-party redaction: `[a line withheld · PAIA s63]`. He writes no reason into the request; if he has one, it is a `safeguarding_concern_note`. He cannot delay a pack past the due date and cannot refuse one: if he believes the asker should not receive the record at all, the route is the Safeguarding Policy's (the office revokes the link with a recorded reason, SAFEGUARDING §4.5 and lifecycle D13), after which the request is answered by D2's fixed sentence. The requester never sees a step: the status is "Being prepared" from receipt until "Ready".

**D7 · Time.** A request is due **30 calendar days** after it is complete (PAIA s56(1), as I read it; §8 A1). The school aims for **ten working days**; the pilot runbook says so. One extension of up to 30 days (PAIA s57) is allowed only for volume (a boy with six seasons of record) or a necessary consultation, and only by a written notice to the requester before the first due date, with the new date. The family sees three states: "Received {date}; the school will answer by {due}", "Ready", and, if extended, "The school needs until {new date}: {the fixed reason}". No other state is ever shown.

**D8 · Fee.** Confirmation that a record exists (s23(1)(a)) is immediate and free: the request screen says what classes the school keeps about a pupil, in the same words for every pupil. The pack (s23(1)(b)) is **free** for a family in the pilot; the school may set a prescribed fee later through the manual route, never through the app. Q8.

**D9 · Correction (s24).** A correction request names one record (a figure, a date, a field, a note, an answer) and says what is wrong and what is right. Each kind has its door (§5): the family's own edits, the office's edit with evidence, a `scoring_amendment`, a `skill_withdraw()`, the physio's review, the author's withdrawal of a message. Where the school does not agree, the function attaches a **dispute marker** to the record (`record_dispute`), read with the record by everyone who reads the record, saying "A correction was requested on {date} and was not made." (s24(2)(b)). The marker is never on a public page (the match record is shared, and the public rule says nothing about one child), and never on another child's row.

**D10 · What is logged, what is kept.** The request and the decision are rows in `access_request` (kept **7 years** after closing, with the data event register's period, INCIDENTS §7.4, so the Regulator can be answered years later). The pack file itself is kept **90 days** after release, then deleted (a second copy of a child's record is a second thing to lose); the **manifest** (which sections, which fixed sentences, how many rows per disclosed class, which lines carried the marker) is kept with the request. Every act is an `access_log` row (`access_request.opened/decided/dso_checked/released/collected/extended`), and the pack's assembly is itself a logged read of the child's record under the information officer's uuid with `fields = 'access_pack'`, so it appears in the family's own "Who has seen his record" as "the school office, preparing the copy you asked for" (INCIDENTS D18 item 4: every export is a row).

**D11 · The form.** On screen first: the pack is a page inside the app, behind sign-in and a fresh re-authentication (within ten minutes), with "Save as file" producing one PDF, logged as an export. The file's name and the page's title carry no child's name (`SCRBRD-record-{request short code}.pdf`). The only email ever sent is the pointer: "Your request to {school} is ready. Open SCRBRD to read it." The office may print it for a family that asks, hands it over in person against the request's code, and records `collected`. A pack is **never** emailed as an attachment and never carries a child's name in a subject line, a file name, a URL or a push (NOTIFICATIONS' pointer rule). For a requester without an account (a former pupil, a parent without a link whose standing the office verified on paper), the office prints, or posts a password-protected PDF with the password given by phone; the manual route's checklist says so.

**D12 · A pupil's own request.** What Me shows him is already his answer for his own file, at any age, and the office may print it for him on request (no new disclosure). The **full pack**, which adds the school's access log, is his alone **from eighteen**, as READERS Q14 decided; under eighteen the pack goes to a guardian. One exception already decided (READERS Q14, 30 Oct): the union passport log is his at any age and appears in his Me-printout. Q3 asks whether a pupil of sixteen may ask alone.

## 3 · What never surfaces, and how a refusal is worded

### 3.1 Never in any pack

- **A safeguarding concern, its existence, its reporter, its notes, its shares, a suspension, the lift bar**, and every access-log row whose resource starts `safeguarding` (SAFEGUARDING §1 items 2–3, §4.3). The pack cannot carry them because no function in the pack's path holds `safeguarding.concern.read`, and the DSO's mark (D6) adds nothing *to* the pack.
- **A coach's prose**: `development_note` and coaching points, `technique_review.notes`, the physio's worklist words that are not the clinical record (reconciliation D1; SCRBRD-110 §6.4; GA-I26a §3). Held back everywhere, held back here, by a fixed sentence (§3.3). Q5 asks Kameel to take the position lifecycle Q12 left open.
- **Other children as people**: no other boy's row, name beyond the public label, figure, availability, health, note or message. A shared match event appears as the opener's line in it ("c boy A" becomes "caught", and the scorecard link is to the public page). A team sheet is his own row. A side thread or a lift thread is out entirely.
- **Other adults' personal information**: numbers, emails, addresses, account states, reasons recorded about them (revocation, disable, moderation, withdrawn honour), the reporter of anything, the nominator of anything.
- **Reasons the designs already keep from the family**: the revocation reason (D13), the disable reason (lifecycle §2.5), the never-public mark's reason, the moderation reason (PARENT_MESSAGING D18), the withdrawn honour's reason (RECOGNITION), the departure reason beyond the kind.
- **The platform's internals**: uuids, table names, the reader's name on the access log (categories, INCIDENTS D20; the office gives a name in person from Who Read What).
- **Scorebook photographs** (SCRBRD-120: purged 30 days after confirmation; a page carries other children's names). The fact that a match was imported from a scorebook, and on what date, is in.
- **A child's ID number in full** (READERS Q16 for his own phone; the parent holds `player.identity.read` but a file is a file): the pack says "ends …{last three digits}; the office will confirm the full number in person." Same for both columns.

### 3.2 Never in the request's status or timing

One status word at a time: Received, Being prepared, Ready, Collected, or Needs until {date}. No "waiting for the safeguarding officer", no "under review by", no counter of steps. The due date is set from receipt, the same for everyone. The DSO's mark has a fixed SLA (three working days) inside the ten the office aims for, so it never moves a date on its own.

### 3.3 The fixed sentences

Each is written once in `access_pack.mjs`, appears in every pack in its section, in this wording or Kameel's. The section numbers are PAIA's as I read them, for counsel (§8 A2).

| section | the fixed sentence |
|---|---|
| **Safeguarding** | "Records kept by the school's Designated Safeguarding Officer, if any exist, are not part of this response. They are held under the school's Safeguarding Policy and Cricket South Africa's, and a request about them is made to the Designated Safeguarding Officer, whose card is in the app." |
| **Coaching notes** | "Coaches may write notes for themselves and the school's coaching staff about a player's development. The school does not disclose these notes through a request for access; they are the coaches' working opinions and are kept for two years after a pupil leaves. If you believe a note is wrong, you may ask the information officer to review it." |
| **Conduct** | "Records of conduct, where any exist, are communicated to a pupil's family by the school in its own way and are not part of this response." (the parent's column only; the pupil's column carries his own record, STEP4 Q6) |
| **Wellness check-ins** (the parent's column) | "Daily check-ins a pupil records about how his body feels are shown to him in the app. The school shares them with a parent by a recorded act for a stated period; the shares made to you are listed below. Nothing else of them is in this response." |
| **Other children** | "Other pupils appear in this response as the school's public pages would show them. Their records are their own families' to ask for." |
| **Other adults** | "Staff and other adults appear by their role. Their contact details and the school's records about them are not part of this response." |
| **The audit log** (the pupil's column under eighteen) | not applicable: the under-eighteen pack is the guardian's (D12) |
| **The platform's own records** | "SCRBRD, which runs this app for the school, holds the account you sign in with: your name, email address, sign-in method and sign-in times. A request about those is made to SCRBRD's information officer at {address}, and is answered within the same time." |
| **A refusal of a part** (PAIA ground) | "A part of the record is withheld under section {63 | 66 | 67} of the Promotion of Access to Information Act. You may ask the Information Regulator to review this decision; its details are at the end of this response." |

The last line is the **only** refusal wording the app knows. Section 63 (mandatory protection of a third party's privacy) is the ground for every third-party line, which is why every pack has at least one use of it, and why its presence says nothing. Section 66 (safety of individuals) is available to the information officer on the manual route only, is never selected in the app, and §8 A2 asks counsel whether a s66 refusal may be worded so as not to confirm what it protects. The design's position is that it should never be needed: the same-shape rule and D2's link sentence answer the safeguarding case without a conditional refusal.

## 5 · Correction (s24)

### 5.1 The route

From the pack (every disclosed row has "Something wrong here?") or from Family and Me directly, a correction request names one record and carries two sentences: what is wrong, what is right. It becomes a `correction_request` row, visible to the information officer's queue and to the person whose door it is (below). The family sees: Received, Being looked at, Corrected, Not changed (with the marker attached), or Sent to the scorers (for a figure).

### 5.2 Each kind has its door

| the record | who corrects | how | if they disagree |
|---|---|---|---|
| contact, address, emergency contacts | the family, now | her own edits (built) | — |
| name, date of birth, ID number, boarding house | the office, with evidence | `player` edit under `player.profile.manage`; the old value in the audit | the marker, and the family told what evidence would change it |
| **a ball, a dismissal, a figure** | the scorer or competition organiser through **`scoring_amendment`**, never an edit (GA-I36: a corrected ball reaches the pad, the Match Centre, the public page, careers, reports and the charts, both versions kept) | the office raises the amendment proposal on the family's behalf, citing the request's code; the existing approval route decides | a declined amendment is itself the record of the request (readable with the match's amendments by those who read them); the family is told; **no marker on the public card**, which is the shared record and names no one child's dispute |
| an availability answer | the family or the pupil | declare again; `match_availability_history` keeps both | — |
| an injury row, a review | the physio | a new review row; the old stands struck | the marker, read by every reader of the injury row |
| a skill score (the pupil's request) | the coach of the current side | `skill_withdraw()` with the reason "disputed by the pupil, request {code}", and a new row if he re-rates (AH-D5: never overwritten, the authorship never changes hands) | **a rating is an opinion**: the school does not change a coach's judgement on a family's say; the marker attaches and is read by every reader of the row (coach, assistant, director, the boy) |
| a coaching note | not disclosed, so not correctable from the pack | the information officer's review under the fixed sentence (§3.3); the author may withdraw (`note_withdraw()`) | the marker, read by the note's readers only |
| a disciplinary record (the pupil's request) | the office or principal | per the school's conduct process; the record is edited only by its owner (db/25: a competition's record is the competition's) | the marker |
| an honour or a withdrawn honour | `recognition.decide` | RECOGNITION's route | the marker on the honour, never on the public board |
| a consent's recorded state or date | the office | a new consent row (consents are versioned; nothing is edited) | the marker on the consent row |
| a message in the child thread | the author | withdraw (D17) and write again | a parent may withdraw her own; a staff message she disputes gets the marker on the thread's row |
| a lift or trip row | the office | the existing lift and trip edits | the marker |
| the access log | nobody | a log is never edited (lifecycle: "a log with holes is not a log"); if a row is wrong the information officer writes a dated note on the request, not on the log | — |

### 5.3 The marker

`record_dispute (id, school_id, player_id, record_kind, record_id, correction_request_id, attached_at, attached_by, lifted_at, lifted_by)`. One per record; attached by `correction_request_decide()` with outcome `not_changed`; lifted only by a later `corrected` on the same record. Every read of the record kind renders it: "A correction was requested on {date} and was not made. Ask the school office for the request's code." It is **inside** the platform only; no `public_*()` function reads it (the never-public list gains the table, tested as PUBLIC_DATA §3 is). It travels where the record travels: a passport line with a marker carries the marker to the receiving school (s24(3): third parties who received the information are told where reasonably practicable, and the passport is the one place a child's figure leaves the school).

### 5.4 Time and who decides

A correction is decided within the same 30 days (s24 says "as soon as reasonably practicable"; the design gives it the access window and a ten-day aim). The decider is the record's owner above; the information officer closes the request and is the second reader of any `not_changed`. Separation as D5: the owner of the door cannot be the requester, and a coach who is the opener's father does not decide his son's score.
