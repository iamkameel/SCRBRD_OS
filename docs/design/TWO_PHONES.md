# Two phones scoring one match — the design

**Status:** approved. Kameel answered "All seven as recommended" on the Build Board (2026-11-05, 11:55 UTC). Written by Fable, 2026-11-05, at Kameel's ask of 4 November (CLAUDE.md: "two devices scoring the same match whose logs disagree; which ball stands, how each scorer is told, how the log keeps both versions, and what the official result and the public page read meanwhile"). Slices 1 and 2 are being built for the pilot.
**Source:** a reading list prepared for the design; refs `origin/main`, except `db/132` at `origin/claude/wip-pilot-audit-fixes` (live in production).
**Closes:** pilot item 3's last open part; the readiness finding "two disagreeing phones are not merged"; the no-go "lost scoring events after reconnect, or non-reproducible scorecard totals".
**Reader:** Kameel on a phone first; then the Opus lead; then whoever builds a slice.

---

## 0 · In one paragraph

Almost all of this is built. One phone holds the pen (a leased, numbered token); a second phone may take the pen only by a confirmed take-over or a verified handover, and every ball a phone sends under a pen it no longer holds is **set aside** in `ball_event_quarantine`, never written into the log and never merged. The readiness review's "two disagreeing phones are not merged" is therefore correct and is the *design*, not the defect. The real gap is what happens next: the person who reviews the set-aside balls sees them one at a time with Accept and Reject buttons and nothing beside them to compare against, and **Accept appends the ball at the end of the log** — so a reviewer who "accepts" a second phone's honest account of an over the pen-holder also scored *doubles the over*. That is the one road from "two phones" to "non-reproducible totals", and nothing today closes it. Neither scorer is told in words that a second account exists, the reviewer's reason is thrown away (`p_note` is accepted and never stored), and nothing stops the pen-holder from judging the disagreement himself. The design settles the rule (the pen-holder's book is the match's book; the other phone's is a second scorer's book, kept whole, compared, never merged), the words on each pad, the record both versions leave, what the result and the pages say meanwhile, who decides and who may not, and three build slices, the first two before the pilot.

## 2 · The gap

1. **Review is per ball, blind, and Accept appends.** The reviewer of a second phone's account sees N rows, each with its own Accept and Reject, and nothing beside them says what the pen-holder's log holds at that point of the match. `quarantine_resolve` was built for a *dead* phone's late balls that nobody else recorded (`SCORING_HANDOVER_SPEC.md:107-110`), where appending at the next seq is right. For a second phone that scored the *same* overs, every Accept is a duplicate delivery: the over goes 4 → 8 runs, a wicket falls twice. The screen offers it as the natural "yes". This is the pilot's no-go in one tap.
2. **No comparison exists anywhere.** Nothing lines the second account up against the pen's log, says where the two agree, where they differ and where the pen is missing a ball. The disagreement — the thing a scorer actually wants settled — has no object, no words and no record.
3. **Neither scorer is told.** The pad knows (`rejectedCount`, `sync-engine.mjs:86`, `:667`; the forked pad of spec §8 L166) but says it as a count. The pen-holder is told nothing. The only notice (`late_ball_notice`) fires for a finished match only (`db/132:631`).
4. **The reviewer's reason is lost.** `quarantine_resolve(p_id, p_accept, p_row, p_note)` takes a note (`db/14:40`) and writes it nowhere: the table has `resolved_at`, `resolved_by`, `resolution` and no note (`db/02:178-180`). A rejected second account says "rejected" and not "the pen's ball stands; the other phone had 6, the pen had 4, the book says 4".
5. **The pen-holder may judge his own disagreement.** `cannot_release_your_own` excludes the scorer who *sent* the set-aside ball (`db/14:57-59`), not the scorer who wrote the ball it disagrees with. A director of sport who scored the second innings and holds `scoring.amend.approve` can reject the other phone's account of his own overs.

Nothing in 1–5 needs a change to how balls are written, leased or set aside. All of it is in the review and the words.

## 3 · The scenarios

Two scorers, A on phone A and B on phone B, both adults holding `scoring.start` for the side. The pad shows players' names to the scorer as it does today; nothing below puts a name on a lock screen.

**S1 · Both phones offline, then both reconnect.** A holds the pen (epoch 1) and scores to 8.2, then loses signal; the lease lapses at 90 s. B opens the pad online: `held_elsewhere`. B's pad shows the server's last ball (8.2, with its time) and **Take over scoring**; B presses it: epoch 2, a `takeover` audit row. B scores from 8.3. A, with no signal, keeps tapping 8.3 onwards into his own log; his queue grows. B also loses signal at 10.4 and scores to 12.0 offline. Both come back.
B's phone: its own lapsed lease is its own to take back (`db/126:31-33`); the plain claim gives epoch 3; the epoch rule restamps B's epoch-2 balls to 3 and they are accepted. The log reads A to 8.2, B from 8.3 to 12.0.
A's phone: its probe says the session moved on; `reconcile` finds balls on the server A lacks and balls on A the server lacks → **fork**, no attach, nothing sent (`attach.mjs:224`). The pad lists A's 22 balls the server lacks, writes A's whole log aside on the phone and offers **Send for review** (spec §8 L166); sent, they land in quarantine as `stale_epoch_or_lease` with `submitted_epoch 1, current_epoch 3`. If A's flush beat the probe, the same 22 land there the same way (`events-api.mjs:471-490`). Either way: log = A to 8.2 + B from 8.3; set aside = A's 8.3–12.0. **B's balls stand.** Today the reviewer is then shown 22 Accept buttons.

**S2 · A take-over while the first phone is still scoring offline.** S1 without B's outage. The boundary is the `takeover` row: everything A sent *before* it and the server has is the match; everything A recorded *after* it is a second account. The one honest question is the gap between the server's last ball at the take-over (8.2) and B's first ball: if B started at 8.3 having read the banner, A's unsent 8.1–8.2 (had he any) and B's 8.1–8.2 would both exist; if B misread and started at 8.5, A's 8.3–8.4 are **balls the pen is missing**, and releasing them is right. The comparison of §4 R4 is what tells these apart; nothing today does.

**S3 · A handover where the first phone held unsent balls.** Refused before it starts: arming needs `pendingCount == 0` (`scoring-session.mjs:252`; spec §4 step 1), and A cannot arm at all without signal. The pad says "8 balls not yet uploaded. Move to better signal before handing over." If A cannot, B waits for the lease to lapse and S2 applies. The variant that does happen — A arms with nothing pending, B verifies and takes the pen, A then taps one more ball under the old epoch — is set aside on A's next flush and is exactly what `smoke-browser-handover-offline.mjs` proves.

**S4 · A phone comes back after the match was finished.** B finished the match from the pad (`scoring.finalise`, db/130). A's phone comes back with balls: `scoring_lease_check` says `match_complete`, every ball is set aside with that reason (`events-api.mjs:482-486`), `late_ball_notice` tells A and the home office (`db/132:620-663`), and the result is `provisional · ball_held` until someone decides (`db/114:570-572`). Finish was *not* refused for A's lapsed lease — only a live lease refuses (`db/132:604-609`, Kameel 4 Nov) — so this will happen on a Saturday with a flat battery. If A's balls are the end of an innings the pen never had, release changes the result: an official result lapses by D9 and both schools are told. The comparison view is the same as S1's; the decision has the same three answers.

**S5 · Two phones record the same delivery differently.** At 11.3 the pen (B) has "4 runs, striker X facing bowler Y"; A's set-aside row has "6 runs". B's stands in the log. The comparison shows the two side by side, marked *differs*. The reviewer, who is neither A nor B, reads the paper book or asks the umpires, then takes one of three decisions (R5): the pen stands (A's row rejected, counterpart named, note "book says 4"); the pen missed a ball (release, the existing path); or the other phone was right — the pen's ball needs an amendment (request → someone else approves → void, as today), and A's row is rejected with the amendment named. The ball is never swapped in place.

## 4 · The rules

**R1 · Which ball stands.** The scorer holding the pen is the match's scorer for the deliveries he writes under a live lease at the current epoch; his log is the match's book. A phone that writes under a pen it does not hold is a second scorer's book. In the Laws two scorers are appointed, record independently and check with each other, and where their books disagree they consult and then ask the umpires — the books are never averaged. SCRBRD does the same: the pen's book is the record, the second account is kept whole beside it, and a disagreement is decided by a person who kept neither book, against the paper book and the umpires. No clock, no "latest wins", no vote.

**R2 · Where the pen passed.** The boundary of a take-over is the `takeover` audit row (`db/132:762-767`: the old device, the new, when the lease lapsed, the server's last ball and seq). A handover's is `handover_complete`. The comparison starts there: a second account's balls before the boundary that the server already has are duplicates; after it they are a second account; the server's last seq at the boundary is where "the pen missed a ball" can be true.

**R3 · The words on the pad.** A sheet, not a toast, in the pad's own voice, no person named:
- *the second phone, at reconnect (fork, or balls set aside):* "**Another phone scored this match while this one had no signal.** 22 balls on this phone are not in the match record. They are kept here and set aside on the server for review — nothing was lost, and none of them was added to the score. Someone who approves corrections will compare the two accounts." Buttons: **Send for review** (when not yet sent) · **Done**. The pad then shows the server's log, as a forked pad does today, and the scorer may read but not score until he claims or takes over by the ordinary doors.
- *the pen-holder, at the next sync after a second account lands:* one line in the session badge, "Your scoring stands · 22 balls from another phone set aside for review", with a tap to the same wording as above. Nothing to do.
- *push and lock screen (NOTIFICATIONS D1/D2, the db/132 pattern):* title "Second account set aside · Westville 1st XI v Kearsney", body "Balls from a second phone were set aside for review, not added to the scorecard." To the scorer who sent them (his alone), the home office under `fixture.update`, and the approvers under `scoring.amend.approve` at school scope. Once per match and reader while the first notice stands. No child, no ball, no scorer's name; a pupil scorer's notice honours quiet hours.

**R4 · How the log keeps both versions.** The pen's version is `ball_event`, immutable. The second account is the `ball_event_quarantine` rows, one per ball, body and fingerprint whole, never deleted (no DELETE policy; resolution is a stamp). What is added: every resolution writes a `scoring_audit` row `second_account_resolved` with `quarantine_id`, `resolution`, `counterpart_key` (the pen's ball it was compared with, or null for "pen missed this ball"), `note`, `submitted_epoch`, `current_epoch`; `audit_log()` words it ("Set aside a second account of 11.3: the pen's ball stands"). A person can therefore read, for any ball, who wrote the one that stands, who wrote the other, who decided between them and why. The losing version is never a void: a void is a correction to the match's book, and the second account was never in it.

**R5 · The three decisions, and the comparison that offers them.** The review screen folds the pen's log and the second account separately (the same `deriveInnings`) and lines them up by innings and legal-ball ordinal, extras attached to the legal ball before them. Each row reads *agrees*, *differs* or *pen has no ball here*; where the counts drift (one phone has an extra wide) the view says from which ball the accounts stop lining up and the reviewer decides by over. The alignment is a reading aid; the decision is the person's. Per row, or for a run of rows:
1. **Pen stands** — reject, with `counterpart_key` and a note. For every row that *agrees*, one tap closes them all ("Pen stands for the 19 that agree"): identical content needs no reading, and the review is of the differences.
2. **Pen missed this ball** — release: the existing accept path, Laws judged, `recovered`, appended at the next seq (which puts a mid-innings ball at the innings' end: totals right, the over strip wrong at that point; said on the screen, as today).
3. **The other phone was right** — opens an amendment request on the pen's ball (`scoring.amend.request`, approved by a second hand under `db/38`'s lock); the set-aside row is rejected with the amendment id in its note. Never a swap in place.
Nothing is decided by default, and nothing times out: `ball_held` blocks confirmation until a person has been through it.

**R6 · Who may decide.** By capability only: `scoring.amend.approve` over the match (as today), and the decider is **neither book's scorer**: not `scorer_user_id` of the set-aside row (today's `cannot_release_your_own`) and not `scorer_user_id` of any live `ball_event` in this match (new refusal `you_scored_this_match`). The scorer holding the pen is a party to the disagreement and may not settle it. A releaser is already a hand on the log for D5 and may not confirm the result; a rejecter wrote nothing and may.

**R7 · What the result, the Match Centre and the public page read meanwhile.** All three read `ball_event_live`: the pen's book, exactly as it would read with no second phone. The result is `provisional`, `blocked_by: ball_held` (built). Words: public and parents see **Provisional** and nothing more (OFFICIAL_RESULT D11); staff who may read the quarantine see "Provisional · 22 balls from a second phone await review"; the organiser's 48 h nudge applies unchanged. "Under review" is not used for this: it is D7's word for a dispute between schools. Standings count the provisional result (D10). A release or an approved amendment after confirmation lapses it and tells both schools (D9, built).

**R8 · Finish.** db/132's rule stands: a live lease elsewhere refuses Finish; a lapsed one does not, because a phone that is gone must not hold the result hostage. One addition: when the match's audit holds a `takeover` row and the finisher's device is not the one that was taken from, the result screen says "Another phone scored part of this match. If it still has unsent balls they will be set aside for review after the finish." — a line, not a block.

## 5 · Decisions (Kameel, 2026-11-05: all seven as recommended)

1. **The pen-holder's book is the match's book; the other phone's is a second scorer's book, kept whole, compared, never merged** (R1, R2). *Recommended.* Alternative: merge by time, or let the reviewer stitch the two logs together ball by ball. Why not: a stitched log looks authoritative and nobody can later say which phone each over came from; the spec has forbidden it since day one, the database enforces it, and the readiness finding that "two phones are not merged" was reporting the design working. The thing to build is the comparison and the words, not a merge.
2. **Who settles a disagreement: anyone holding the approval capability who kept neither book — not the scorer who sent the set-aside balls and not the scorer of any ball in the match's log** (R6). *Recommended.* Alternative: exclude only the two scorers' overlapping stretch, or only the sender (today). Why: the pen-holder is a party, and at a small school the director of sport often scores; the rule must be one a scorer accepts without reading code. The cost is that a match scored by the only approver needs another approver (another staff member holding the capability, or the platform) — say so on the screen rather than bend the rule.
3. **The agreeing balls close in one tap** (R5.1). *Recommended.* Alternative: every set-aside ball is read and decided one by one. Why: a second phone that scored four overs produces 24 rows of which perhaps one differs; a reviewer made to tap 24 times on a Saturday evening will tap Accept, and Accept doubles the over. The review is of the differences; the agreeing rows are the proof that the two books agree.
4. **"The other phone was right" goes through an amendment, two hands, never a swap** (R5.3). *Recommended.* Alternative: let the reviewer replace the pen's ball with the other phone's directly. Why not: that is one person changing the match's book, which the amendment rule (requester and approver are different people) exists to prevent; and a replaced ball breaks the promise that `ball_event` is never edited.
5. **Both scorers are told on their pads in a sheet, the office and the approvers by a notice that names the fixture by its sides and nothing else; the public sees "Provisional" only** (R3, R7). *Recommended.* Alternative: tell only the office, as the late-ball notice does today; or tell the public that a review is open. Why: the second scorer must know his afternoon's work is safe and not in the score, or he will re-enter it; the approvers must know there is something to review or `ball_held` blocks confirmation for ever; the public must never learn that two adults disagree about a boy's innings.
6. **Finish stays as db/132 made it (a live lease blocks, a lapsed one does not), with one warning line when a take-over happened in the match** (R8). *Recommended.* Alternative: refuse Finish while any phone that ever held the pen has not reported an empty queue. Why not: a phone that is dead or at the bottom of a kit bag would hold the result for the weekend; the set-aside path and the provisional label already carry the late balls safely to review.
7. **Slices 1 and 2 before the pilot; slice 3 may follow in the first fortnight** (§6). *Recommended.* Alternative: slice 1 only (the words), review stays per ball. Why not: slice 1 alone leaves the Accept-doubles-the-over road open, which is the only route from this problem to the no-go.

## 6 · The build, in slices

Each slice is one Opus builder, one PR, pushed as it goes. Every db change is a new `db/NN_*.sql`; `db/14` and `db/37` are frozen, so `quarantine_resolve` is replaced whole, as db/37 replaced db/14's.

**Slice 1 · The second account is proven to arrive, and both scorers are told.** No change to how balls are written or set aside.
- Pad: the fork sheet's words (R3) over the existing forked-pad path; the pen-holder's badge line; the forked pad's **Send for review** confirmed to deliver to quarantine (spec §8 says it is built; the pad code is outside this reading list, so the walk is where it is proven).
- Server: `second_account_notice()` beside `late_ball_notice()` — an AFTER INSERT on `ball_event_quarantine` for `stale_epoch_or_lease` rows from a device that is not the holder, once per match and reader, to the sender, the office (`fixture.update`) and the approvers (`scoring.amend.approve`), fixture by sides.
- Proof: **walk** `tools/smoke-browser-two-phones.mjs` — A scores offline past 8.2, B takes over (confirmed) and scores to 12.0, A reconnects: A's pad shows the sheet with the right count, A's balls are all in quarantine as `stale_epoch_or_lease`, `ball_event` holds no row from A with `epoch < current` after the take-over's `last_seq`, `match_live_score` equals B's fold, the notice rows exist and name no player. **db/99 check:** the notice function's grants and its words contain no `striker_id` lookup.

**Slice 2 · The comparison, the three decisions, the record, the separation.**
- Server: `GET /api/matches/:id/second-account` — the alignment of R5 (folds in the API, not SQL, as the Laws are); a new `db/NN_second_account.sql` replacing `quarantine_resolve` whole with `p_counterpart text` added, the `you_scored_this_match` refusal after `cannot_release_your_own`, and the `scoring_audit` row `second_account_resolved` written on every resolution (note stored at last); `audit_log()` gains the words; a bulk route that resolves a list of ids as "pen stands" inside one transaction, same function, same checks per row.
- Screen: `quarantine.jsx` becomes the comparison (pen's ball · other phone's ball · agrees/differs/missing) with the three decisions and the one-tap close for agreeing rows; 12 px floor, 44 px taps, no colour-only state.
- Proof: **db/99 checks** — resolve by the pen-holder refused `you_scored_this_match`; by the sender refused `cannot_release_your_own`; by a third approver ok; the audit row present with `counterpart_key` and note; `match_confirmable` returns `ball_held` while one row is open and not after; a bulk "pen stands" writes no `ball_event` row; `quarantine_resolve` is SECURITY DEFINER, pinned, not PUBLIC. **Walk** — the Slice 1 walk continued: the reviewer closes the 21 agreeing rows in one tap, decides the one that differs as "pen stands", totals unchanged before and after.

**Slice 3 · The words on the result and the Finish line.**
- `StateLabel` staff `why` for `ball_held` from a second account ("22 balls from a second phone await review"), gated as I36 gates correction words; the Finish warning line of R8 when a `takeover` row exists.
- Proof: **walk** asserting the public page reads "Provisional" alone and the staff label carries the count; **db/99** unchanged (`ball_held` is db/114's).

Not in these slices, named so they are not lost: the released ball's position (appended at the innings' end; totals right, over strip wrong at that point — db/14's standing behaviour, a later design if the pilot shows it matters); the server trusting the pad's `pendingCount` at arm (a malformed client could arm with balls unsent; the set-aside path still catches them); retention of set-aside rows for a leaving family (POPIA, spec §10, inherits the tenant policy).
